3 · The big picture

The 7 weeks in one minute each

WEEK 1

What is economics

Resources are scarce, so we must choose. Every choice costs the next-best thing you give up (opportunity cost). The 12 principles come in three groups: 4 about individual choice, 5 about interaction, 3 about the whole economy.

scarcityopportunity costmarginal decisionsequilibriumefficiency vs equity
WEEK 2

Models, PPF and trade

The PPF shows the best combinations a country can make. A point on it is efficient, inside it is wasteful, outside it is impossible. Whoever has the lower opportunity cost has the comparative advantage, and both sides gain from trade.

PPFcomparative advantagecircular flowpositive vs normativeslope, derivative
WEEK 3

Supply and demand

Price up → people buy less (law of demand). A change in the good's own price is a movement along the curve. Anything else (income, tastes, prices of other goods…) shifts the curve. The market settles where Qd = Qs.

shift vs movementequilibriumshortage / surplusnormal vs inferior
WEEK 4

The market strikes back

Governments set ceilings (max price → shortage), floors (min price → surplus) and quotas (max quantity → wedge and quota rent). All of them cause deadweight loss: trades that would help both sides never happen.

consumer surplusproducer surplusprice ceiling / floorquota rentDWL
WEEK 5

Elasticity

How strongly buyers react to a price change. e > 1 = elastic (a price rise lowers revenue), e < 1 = inelastic (a price rise raises revenue), e = 1 = unit-elastic. The midpoint method gives the same answer in both directions.

% method vs midpointtotal revenue testdR/dP = Q(1 − e)determinants
WEEK 6

Behind the supply curve

Extra workers add less and less (diminishing returns). So costs rise: TC = FC + VC, MC = ΔTC/ΔQ, ATC = TC/Q. MC cuts ATC and AVC at their lowest points, and that lowest ATC is the minimum-cost output.

short vs long runMPLTC, MC, ATC, AVC, AFCU-shaped ATC
WEEK 7

Perfect competition

Many small firms selling the same thing, so each is a price-taker (P = MR). A firm produces where P = MC. If P is below min ATC it makes a loss. If P is below min AVC it shuts down. The firm's supply curve is MC above min AVC.

price-takerP = MR = MCbreak-even (min ATC)shut-down (min AVC)
4 · Calculations

The 6 calculations you must be able to do

These are the easiest marks to win, and the easiest to lose. Learn each recipe, then cover the solution and do the example yourself.

1

Elasticity: simple % and midpoint

Q25, Q26 · Week 5 slides 5–6
  1. %ΔQ = change in Q ÷ starting Q × 100. %ΔP is the same for price.
  2. e = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP (ignore the minus sign).
  3. Midpoint: divide by the average of the two values, not the starting one.
  4. Classify: e > 1 elastic, e < 1 inelastic, e = 1 unit-elastic. Check with revenue R = P × Q.
His example

Price goes $0.90 → $1.10 and quantity goes 1,100 → 900.

%ΔQ%ΔPe
Start at A ($0.90)200/1,100 = 18.18%0.20/0.90 = 22.22%0.82
Start at B ($1.10)200/900 = 22.22%0.20/1.10 = 18.18%1.22
Midpoint200/1,000 = 20%0.20/1.00 = 20%1.00

Revenue: 0.90 × 1,100 = $990 = 1.10 × 900. Revenue doesn't change, so demand is unit-elastic, which matches the midpoint answer. The simple method gives two different answers, and that is exactly why we use the midpoint.

2

The cost table

Q27 (+ Q10, Q21–Q22) · Week 6 slide 16
  1. TC = FC + VC. FC is the same at every quantity, even at Q = 0.
  2. MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ: how much TC rises for one more unit.
  3. ATC = TC/Q · AVC = VC/Q · AFC = FC/Q. Check: ATC = AVC + AFC.
  4. The lowest ATC is the minimum-cost output. MC crosses ATC exactly there.

3

Equilibrium, shortage and surplus from a table

Q28 · Week 3 slides 21, 22, 6
  1. Find the price where Qd = Qs. That is the equilibrium.
  2. Above it: Qs > Qd → surplus = Qs − Qd → the price falls.
  3. Below it: Qd > Qs → shortage = Qd − Qs → the price rises.
  4. Supply decreases → S shifts left → P up, Q down. Demand increases → D shifts right → P up, Q up.
His example: concert tickets

Equilibrium: $250 and 8,000 tickets.

At $350: Qs 8,800 vs Qd 5,000 → surplus 3,800 → the price falls.

At $150: Qd 15,000 vs Qs 5,000 → shortage 10,000 → the price rises.

4

Comparative advantage and trade

Q29–Q31 · Week 2 slide 25
  1. Opportunity cost of 1 X = (max Y) ÷ (max X). Build a 2×2 table.
  2. Compare across people in the same row. The lower opportunity cost has the comparative advantage.
  3. Each specializes in its advantage good.
  4. The deal works if the price lies between the two opportunity costs. Both gain if their after-trade bundle is outside their own PPF.
His example: Tom and Hank

Tom: 40 fish or 30 coconuts → 1 fish costs 3/4 coconut. Hank: 1 fish costs 2 coconuts.

3/4 < 2 → Tom has the advantage in fish, and Hank has it in coconuts.

A workable price for 1 fish is between 3/4 and 2 coconuts. For the PPF line test, use x/a + y/b: = 1 on the line, < 1 inside, > 1 outside.

5

Ceilings, floors, quotas: gaps and deadweight loss

Q13, Q14, Q16 · Week 4
  1. Ceiling (below equilibrium): shortage = Qd − Qs at the ceiling price.
  2. Floor (above equilibrium): surplus = Qs − Qd at the floor price.
  3. Quota: wedge = demand price − supply price at the quota. Quota rent = wedge × quota.
  4. DWL = ½ × (price gap at the traded Q) × (Qe − traded Q).
His three examples

6

Perfect competition: profit, break-even, shut-down

Q23, Q24, Q12 · Week 7
  1. Price-taker: P = MR. Produce where P = MC.
  2. Profit = (P − ATC) × Q.
  3. Break-even price = min ATC. Below it there is a loss, and in the long run firms leave.
  4. Shut-down price = min AVC. Below it, stop now (your loss is only FC).
His example: the tomato farm

At P = $18 the farm makes 5 bushels (MC = 18), with ATC = $14.40.

Profit = (18 − 14.40) × 5 = $18.

Break-even = min ATC = $14. Shut-down = min AVC = $10.

At P = $12: a loss, but 12 > 10, so it keeps producing now and exits in the long run.

9 · Exam day

Tips that save marks

Write formula → numbers → answerEven if the arithmetic goes wrong, the method still earns marks.
Draw a graph for every "explain"Label the axes (P, Q), the curves, the equilibrium point, and the arrows for shifts.
Use his words"Price-taker", "deadweight loss", "opportunity cost", "ceteris paribus". He wrote the slides, so his terms score best.
Say the directionDon't stop at "price changes": write "the price rises because there is a shortage".
Shift or movement?The good's own price changes → movement along the curve. Anything else changes → the curve shifts.
Comparative advantage trapCompare opportunity costs across people for the same good, not two goods for one person.
Elasticity trapThe simple method gives a different answer depending on the starting point. Name the start point, or use the midpoint.
Shut-down vs break-evenShut-down uses AVC (short run). Break-even uses ATC (long run). Don't swap them.
