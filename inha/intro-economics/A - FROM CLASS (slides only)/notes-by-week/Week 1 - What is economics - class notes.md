## 1. Big picture

- **What economics is [S11–S13].** Economics is "(resources) a social science concerned with the production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services" and "(participants) … how individuals, businesses, governments, and nations make choices about how to allocate resources", based on the assumption that humans "act with rational behaviour". It is a "Scientific study on Economy" asking about jobs, production, distribution, consumption, and why some countries are rich and others poor. "All these questions are based on a set of common principles." Economics belongs to the Social Sciences.
- **The logic that organizes the whole lecture [S14 → S15].** Individuals make choices → "my decision is shaped by decisions by other people, our choices interact" → "Interactions as a whole, economy-wide interactions". These three levels are the three groups of the **Twelve Economic Principles: 4 on individual choices + 5 on interaction + 3 on economy-wide interactions.**
- **Branches and background [S16–S18].** Microeconomics (how individuals and firms make decisions) vs. macroeconomics (the economy as a whole), plus 14 other branches. The slides list topics to know "Before we get into the 12 principles" and introduce Adam Smith (*Wealth of Nations*, 1776, and the self-interest quote) and Alfred Marshall (*Principles of Economics*, 1890).
- **I. Individual choice [S19–S32].** Resources (labor, "capital", land and raw materials, entrepreneurial ability, and time) are scarce because they are finite, so we must make choices. Every choice has an opportunity cost, "the value of the next-best alternative" ("no such thing as a free lunch"). This applies to students, firms and governments, and it differs from person to person (the $1000 iPhone). "How much?" decisions are made at the margin: ⭐ hire one more worker if `w < p × MP_L`.
- **II. Interaction [S33–S51].** There are gains from trade because of specialization, and trade is a mutual gain ("MB > MC!"). Trade promotes growth in 3 ways:
  - allocation: goods, resources and labor move to where they are valued
  - specialization and comparative advantage (the country list)
  - economies of scale: Average Cost = Total Cost / Quantity

  The other four interaction principles: markets move toward equilibrium; resources should be used efficiently (efficiency vs. equity); markets usually lead to efficiency; when they don't, government intervention can improve welfare (side effects, monopoly, goods like drugs).
- **III. Economy-wide interactions [S52].** One person's spending is another person's income. Overall spending can get out of line with productive capacity. Government can change overall spending through its spending policy, tax policy and control of money.
- **Key terms [S53].** 15 terms in two columns: individual-choice terms on the left, interaction terms on the right (see §5).

### 4.2 ⭐ Marginal hiring rule [S32]

> **Hire one more worker if `w < p × MP_L`.** The slide writes it as `w < p * MP_L`.

| Symbol | Meaning on the slide |
|---|---|
| `w` | wage = **marginal cost**: "The cost when I hire one more worker" |
| `MP_L` | **marginal product of labor**: "This increased product by this worker" |
| `p` (also written `P`) | market price per unit: "already decided in the market from a price-taker's perspectives" |
| `p × MP_L` | **marginal benefit**: "The benefit I can get when I hire one more. (= increased product x price)" |

- General form on the slide: "if my marginal cost is less than my marginal benefit" → do it. The same comparison appears on S35: "MB > MC!"
- By the same logic, if `w > p × MP_L` the hiring condition is not met. 🔎 Look up in Track B: the case `w = p × MP_L` (the slide doesn't cover it).

### 4.3 Average cost / economies of scale [S47]

> **Average Cost = Total Cost / Quantity**

- Total Cost "includes per-unit costs, but also includes Fixed Costs".
- A larger Quantity (the denominator) gives a lower Average Cost, i.e. "A lower per-unit cost" = economies of scale [S45–S47].

- **Scarcity → choice:** "All resources are "scarce" because they are finite. Since resources are scarce, we have to make choices." [S24]
- **Comparative advantage:** you have it if you can produce a good "at lower opportunity cost relative to other countries" [S44].

## 6. Worked examples from class

### 6.1 ⭐ Marginal decision — should I hire one more worker? [S32]
1. **Situation:** a marginal decision, "A bit more or a bit less from the situation you are now". The question is "Should I hire one more worker?"
2. **Marginal cost (MC):** what one more worker costs, MC = `w` (the wage).
3. **Marginal benefit (MB):** what one more worker brings in. His extra output is `MP_L`, sold at market price `p` per unit, so MB = `p × MP_L` ("= increased product x price").
4. **The price is given:** "P is already decided in the market from a price-taker's perspectives". The firm only decides whether to hire.
5. **Compare:** "if my marginal cost is less than my marginal benefit, i.e. `w < p * MP_L`, then I should hire one more worker".
6. **The other way round:** if `w > p × MP_L`, the extra worker costs more than the value he adds, so the hiring condition is not met. (The slide doesn't cover `w = p × MP_L`; 🔎 Track B.)

Illustration (my numbers, not on the slide): `p` = $10 per unit, `MP_L` = 20 units, so MB = 10 × 20 = $200.

| Wage `w` | Compare with `p × MP_L` = $200 | Decision by the rule |
|---|---|---|
| $150 | 150 < 200 | Hire (MB is $50 more than MC) |
| $250 | 250 > 200 | Condition not met, so don't hire |

### 6.2 Opportunity cost — what is the true cost of college? [S29]
The tools from the slides: OC = "The value of the next-best alternative" [S28], and "The real cost of something is what you must give up to get it." [S19]

| Expense | Slide's question | Reasoning with the slide's definition |
|---|---|---|
| Tuition and Books | "How else would you have spent the money?" | Whatever else you would have spent the money on (the next-best use) is what you give up, so that is the OC. "Opportunity cost is often … expressed in market prices" [S28]. |
| Studying | "What would you be doing instead? Would you be working?" | Time is a resource [S23] and "Every activity has the opportunity cost of time" [S27]. If you would otherwise be working, the work you give up is the OC of studying. |
| Housing and Food | "Would you still need them?" | You would need housing and food with or without college. You don't give them up *because of* college, so by the "what you must give up to get it" logic they are not really a cost of choosing college. |

The takeaway on the slide: "For each, what are the next best alternatives?"

### 6.3 Opportunity cost for firms and governments [S30]

| Choice made | OC on the slide (the next-best alternative given up) | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| New automated machinery | More workers. | Money spent on machinery is no longer available for the next-best use, hiring more workers. |
| Sheet rock for larger houses | Using it in more schools or hospitals | Sheet rock used in larger houses can't also go into schools or hospitals. |

This follows "Choosing one thing means we're giving up an alternative." [S26] and "The next best alternative is always their OC." [S30]

### 6.4 The $1000 iPhone — opportunity cost differs for everyone [S31]

| | Person A | Person B |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings per week | $1000 | $5000 |
| Price of the iPhone | $1000 | $1000 |
| iPhone as a share of a week's earnings | 1000 ÷ 1000 = 1 → **100%** (a whole week) | 1000 ÷ 5000 = 0.2 → **20%** (one-fifth of a week) |

- The price is the same, but A gives up a whole week of earnings while B gives up one-fifth of a week. For A that is a 5× larger share (100% ÷ 20% = 5).
- The slide's conclusion: "Opportunity costs are relative. They differ for everyone."

### 6.5 Economies of scale — average cost [S47]
1. Total Cost = Fixed Costs + the per-unit costs of all units ("includes per-unit costs, but also includes Fixed Costs").
2. Average Cost = Total Cost / Quantity.
3. As Quantity grows, the fixed costs are spread over more units. "quantity – in the denominator – if larger, will result in a lower Average Cost".

Illustration (my numbers; I assume the per-unit cost stays the same): fixed costs = $1,000 and per-unit cost = $2.

| Quantity | Total Cost = 1,000 + 2 × Q | Average Cost = TC / Q |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | 1,200 | $12.00 |
| 1,000 | 3,000 | $3.00 |
| 10,000 | 21,000 | $2.10 |

- How this links to trade [S46]: trade → larger consumer base → higher quantity demanded → large-scale production → lower per-unit cost.

  - Equity is less well-defined because "people can disagree about what's "fair,"" so "equity isn't as well-defined a concept as efficiency".
- **SA7** [S36]:
  - Expands resource allocation → "More people get the goods and services they want."
  - Specialization and comparative advantage → "Makes larger production and consumption levels possible."
  - Encourages large-scale production methods → "Economies of scale: Large-scale production lowers per-unit cost".

**Calculations**
- **C1** [S32]: MB = `p × MP_L` = 4 × 50 = **$200** per day.
  - (a) `w` = 180 < 200, so **hire**. The worker adds $200 of output for a $180 wage, a $20 gain.
  - (b) `w` = 220 > 200, so the rule `w < p × MP_L` fails: **don't hire**. The cost is $20 more than the benefit.
  - (c) Hire only if **`w` < $200** per day.
- **C2** [S31]:
  - A: 1,500 ÷ 300 = **5 weeks** of earnings (500% of a week's pay).
  - B: 1,500 ÷ 3,000 = **0.5 week** (50%).
  - The same price is a 10× larger share for A. This shows "Opportunity costs are relative. They differ for everyone."
- **C3** [S47]:
  - (a) TC = 60,000 + 5 × 2,000 = 70,000, so AC = 70,000 ÷ 2,000 = **$35**.
  - (b) TC = 60,000 + 5 × 20,000 = 160,000, so AC = 160,000 ÷ 20,000 = **$8**.
  - (c) **Economies of scale.** The fixed costs per unit fall from 60,000 ÷ 2,000 = $30 to 60,000 ÷ 20,000 = $3, while the $5 per-unit cost stays the same (30 + 5 = 35; 3 + 5 = 8). As the slide says, a larger quantity in the denominator gives a lower Average Cost.

**Explain with an example**
- **E1** [S37–S40, S36]:
  - **Goods:** "Voluntary exchanges like trade move goods from people who value them less to people who value them more". "Secondhand markets are a good example of goods gaining value just by being reallocated." [S38]
  - **Resources:** "Trade moves resources to where they don't exist". "The United States doesn't have a lot of bauxite. It must import most of its supply from Jamaica." Bauxite is needed for aluminum, cars and electronics. [S39]
  - **Labor:** immigration is "A voluntary allocation of labor". A Jamaican electronics engineer moves to the US for a better-paying job, and an American sugarcane producer moves to Jamaica to start a sugar farm. [S40]
  - Result: "More people get the goods and services they want." [S36]
- **E2** [S25–S30]:
  - The saying "is often associated with American economist, Milton Friedman. It describes opportunity cost." "Every choice has at least one alternative that we passed up." [S25]
  - Opportunity cost = "The value of the next-best alternative" [S28].
  - Example (college) [S29]: the tuition money could have been spent on something else, and studying time could have been spent working. Even activities with no monetary cost have "the opportunity cost of time" [S27].
  - Firms face it too: buying new automated machinery has the OC of "More workers" [S30].
- **E3** [S34, S41–S44, S36]:
  - Specialization means "each of us can perform the tasks we do best" [S42]. Through trade "people can get more of what they want … than they could if they tried to be self-sufficient", and "This increase in output is due to specialization" [S34].
  - For nations: "every nation has at least one relative advantage in production". "Geography, infrastructure, and labor skill may not be equal between nations, but they all converge to help nations excel in something" [S43]. Examples: Uzbekistan – Cotton yarn, silk waste; Kazakhstan – Crude oil, petrol gas; Ukraine – sunflower oil, corn.
  - Comparative advantage is "All about Opportunity Cost": producing a good "at lower opportunity cost relative to other countries" [S44].
  - Result: trade "Makes larger production and consumption levels possible" [S36].
