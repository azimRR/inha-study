Islam is an Arabic word meaning self-submission to God, obedience, and peace. The Koran and the hadiths describe the basis of Islamic teachings. The Qur'an is a holy book, believed to be the word of God as dictated to Muhammad by the archangel Gabriel and written down in Arabic. It contains Islamic principles, beliefs, legal and moral principles. It consists of 114 units (surahs) and several verses (ayats). Sunnah is a collection of hadiths. The Prophet Muhammad's words and actions are summarized in the form of hadiths. Hadiths are the primary source after the Qur'an. The Shari'ah is Islamic canonical law and it based on the teachings of the Koran and the traditions of the Prophet (Hadith and Sunna) and also other important sources. The Shari’a is a system of legal, ethical principles and practical requirements in Islam. 
There are five basic religious acts in Islam, that known as 'The Pillars of Islam' (arkan al-Islam; also arkan ad-din, "pillars of religion"), which are considered obligatory for all believers: (1) the creed (shahadah), (2) daily prayers (salat), (3) almsgiving (zakat), (4) fasting during Ramadan and (5) the pilgrimage to Mecca (hajj) at least once in a lifetime. 
The main branches of Islam are Sunnis, Shi'ism, and Khawarijs (Kharijites). Kharijites are members of the group that appeared in the first century during the First Fitna, the crisis of leadership after the death of Muhammad (May God honor him and grant him peace). Khawarijs was divided into many subdivisions in the Middle Ages and then disappeared. Only the Ibodis were survived as a branch and remainder. Sunni and Shia sects differ from each other in terms of authority (Sunni – caliphate, Shi'ism – imamah), religious ceremonies and traditions. The Sunni branch of Islam stipulates that, as a head of state, a caliph should be elected by Muslims or their representatives. Followers of Shia Islam believe a caliph should be an Imam chosen by God from the Ahl al-Bayt (“Family of the House”, Muhammad’s direct descendants). In simpler terms, the Sunni favour election while the Shia favour bloodline. There are some sects in Shi’ism: Karmats, Ishmaelites, Zaidids and others. Shiites recognize the Koran as divine, they accept the hadiths related to Ali, and recognize the imam's authority (imamah). Shiites make up around 10 percent of the world's Muslims. Sunnism is a world-wide sect, with about 90% of Muslims. There are four main legal schools in Sunnism: Hanafi, Shafi’ite, Malikite and Hanbalite. 
Under the flag of Islam, Arabic tribal groups united in a vast territory, with a single centralized Arab caliphate. During the medieval and modern period four major caliphates existed:
1) Rashidun Caliphate (632-661)
2) Ummayad Caliphate (661-750)
3) Abbasid Caliphate (750-1258)
4) Ottoman Empire (1517-1924)
Along with the Arab invasion, Movarounnahr also embraced Islam. The Arab conquest of Central Asia can be divided into two historical periods. In late of the seventh century Arab troops captured southern part of Central Asia, such as Balkh, Chaghaniyon, Marv. During early of eighth century Arab army under command of Qutayba ibn Muslim conquered Kesh, Samarqand, Tashkent, O’sh, Eastern Turkestan. 
The Islamic Golden Age is the era in the history of Islam, traditionally dated from the 8th century to the 13th century, during which economic development and cultural works flourished. Early Islamic philosophy began in early 9th century and lasted until late 12th century AD. This period is traditionally understood to have begun during the reign of the Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid (786 - 809) who established House of Wisdom in Baghdad, where scholars from various parts of the world with different cultural backgrounds were mandated to gather and translate all of the world's classical works into the Arabic language. The achievements of this period had a crucial influence on the development of modern philosophy and science, as well as for Renaissance in Europe. In particular, Islamic tradition influenced on all sphere of cultural life in Central Asia. 
Sufism (or Tasawwuf) is often defined as “Islamic mysticism”. Practitioners of Sufism are called “Sufis”. There are different orders (turuq) in Sufism: Yassaviya, Kubraviya, Naqshbandiya and so on. One of main issues of Sufism is dhikr performance, the practice of remembrance of God. There are three main Sufi orders that were originated and developed by Central Asian scholars: Yassawi, Kubrawi and Naqshbandi.  
Khoja Ahmad Yassawi is the founder of Yassawi order and he was born in Sayram in 1093 and died in 1166 in Turkestani city. The basics of Yassawi are as follows: Sufi concepts based on Tawhid (oneness of God); absolute dependence on the Sunnah and Sharia; the order that based on Sharia; sedate (khilva) and remembrance. In Yassawi, special attention was paid to the acquisition of knowledge, generosity and the struggle against the gust (nafs), and the provision of permissible (halal) labor. The Yassawi dhikr is based on vocal performance. According to Sufism, the true origin of man is universe. Human beings must be brought back to this sacred universe. According to majority scholars, Ahmad Yassawi is the author of “Yassawi hikmatlari”.
Najmiddin Kubra (1145-1221), the founder of the Kubrawiyya Sufi order. He was born in Khiva. Kubra's main body of works concerns the analysis of the visionary experience. He wrote numerous important works discussing the visionary experience, including a Sufi commentary on the Quran that he was unable to complete due to his death in 618/1221.Among the works, "Sharhus-sunna val-masolih", "Usuli ashara", "Favoihul-jamol" are popular. The essence of Kubrawiyya is embodied in these ways.
1) Facing to God by his/her will
2) To abstain from all kind of tastes. 
3) To gain the satisfaction of the God 
4) To love the truth
The Kubrawiyya was not largely popular until after Kubra's death in the 13th century. The Kubrawiyya found great development outside of Central Asia, but its influence and presence only lasted till the 15th/16th century, when it was overshadowed by the Naqshbandiya during the Ottoman Empire, though some nominal followers survived. Kubra died during the Mongol invasion.

Bahauddin Naqshband (1318–1389) was the founder of the Sufi Naqshbandi Order. He was born in the village of Qasr-i-Hinduvan near Bukhara.
The essence of this doctrine is clearly illustrated in the slogan "Dil ba yoru dast ba kor” which literally means – "Heart with God, and hands at work". Man is the result of the grace of Allah and should not forget the world, his heart should always be in Allah, and His hand should be in labor. According to Naqshbandi teaching, Muslims should repeat always the dhikr orally and mentally.

A detailed discussion of the basic principles of Islam by Muslim scholars of the 8th-11th centuries and their discussions on religious and philosophical themes led to philosophical arguments about the nature and attributes of God, his relation to man and the universe. As a result, a new discipline arose within the framework of Muslim scholasticism, called ʻIlm al-kalam (knowledge of kalam), or simply kalam. In fact, the beginning of this new science was put by the Mutazilites. It was they who took a principled rational position in relation to some theological questions.
In this period, critical for the history of Muslim theology, three outstanding personalities manifested themselves in three different parts of the Muslim world: al-Maturidi in Central Asia (Samarkand), al-Ashari in Iraq and al-Tahawi in Egypt. All three tried to reconcile contradictory ideas in the rational direction of the kalam and settle the theological problems of their time, working on a system that would resolve the crisis and would correspond to the general principles of the Quran and Sunnah.
Asharism - the name of the philosophical and religious school of Islam, which developed during the X-XI centuries. This direction pursued not only the purification of Islam from the non-Islamic elements that were embedded in it, but also harmonized the religious consciousness with the religious-philosophical thought of Islam. It established the foundations of orthodox Islamic theology or orthodox kalama as opposed to the rationalistic kalama of the mutazilites.
The main problems on which the Ash'arites were at variance with the Mutazilites are as follows:
1) the concept of God and the nature of His attributes;
2) freedom of the human will;
3) the criterion of truth and the criteria of good and evil;
4) the vision (ruyah) of God;
5) uncreation of the Qur'an;
6) the encumbrance of creations with overwhelming tasks;
7) the promise of reward and the threat of punishment;
8) the rational or irrational basis of God's actions;
9) whether God is obligated to do what is most good for His creatures.
Abu Mansur Muhammad al-Maturidi was born in the village of Maturid in the environs of Samarkand. al-Maturidi was born around 853 AD, that is, in the kingdom of the Abbasid Caliph al-Mutawvakil (847-861), who fought the Mu'tazilite doctrine and supported the traditional faith. 
His books, titled "Kitab at-tawhid" and "Kitab ta'vilot al-Qur’an", are of great importance among the scholars. According to Moturidian teachings – Allah, man, faith, universe, science are the dominant positions. 
Al-Maturidi sharply criticized the Mu'tazilite doctrine of divine justice and unity because it led to the denial of the will and power of God, excluded God as the creator of human actions and made Him subordinate to external coercion and thus not free. 
It is impossible to imagine the history of philosophy, natural history and medicine with Farabi, Beruni and Ibn Sina. Abu Nasr Farabi (873-950)  the great thinker and scientist who received the title of "The second teacher" after Aristotle in the Muslim East. His works include more than 160 works, mainly devoted to the interpretation of natural-scientific and philosophical issues and the analysis of actual issues in these areas. He is one of the first to classify the sciences of his time. He expressed a number of progressive ideas on philosophical issues, and also wrote valuable works on music, mathematics, medicine, Arabic grammar, alchemist, logic. His notable works are “Kitāb al-mūsīqī al-kabīr” ("The Great Book Of Music"), “Arā ahl al-madīna al-fāḍila” ("The Virtuous City"), “Kitāb iḥṣāʾ al-ʿulūm ("On The Introduction Of Knowledge"), “Kitāb iḥṣāʾ al-īqā'āt” ("Classification Of Rhythms"). 
The philosopher interprets the universe in two ways: "Wujudu wājib" (God) and "wujudi mumkin" (all material and spiritual beings), all things are in existence due to "Wujudu wājib" (God). They are interconnected with each other reasonably. There is no result without reason, as well there is no reason without result, says Farabi.
God does not need any other cause or motivation as the first reason. He is an expression of absolute existence and wisdom. It is possible to create "wujudi mumkin" but not itself. All material and spiritual beings is in the constant change, in conflict situations, everything develops according to maturity.

Abu Rayhan Beruni (973-1048) is a great scholar and the great thinker who studied on all the fields of science of that period. He is the author of more than 150 works, 28 of them have reached us. 
He acknowledges that the universe was created by Allah. Abstinence is connected with the first cause. 
Some of his works are devoted to the issue of cognition. There are two reasons for interest in cognition. First of all, the process of cognition is a special flavor. Secondly, the purpose of knowledge is to satisfy the needs of the people. The process of cognition begins with the information that provided by sense organs. They serve as a kind of auxiliary and foundation for mental cognition. The sincerity of knowledge is determined through observation and experimentation. 
Abu Ali ibn Sina (980-1037) is a great scholar and thinker. He was born in in the village of Afshona, near Bukhara and died in Hamadon. The scientific works of Ibn Sina are more than 280 names. They are dedicated to medicine, philosophy, logic, psychology, ethics, music, pharmacology and other areas. Scientist's book "The Book of Healing", "The Canon of Medicine", "The Book of Knowledge" and "Nature" are well-known., Ibn Sino followed Farabi on the interpretation of the universe. In particular, "Wujudu wājib" (God) is the first reason and "wujudi mumkin" (all material and spiritual beings) is created by "Wujudu wājib" – God. 
According to the view of Ibn Sina, the motion is manifested in the following three forms:
1. Accidental motion. The body is within other body, it moves from one place to another jointly, but it does not change its place.
2. Forced motion. It is not the body's own motion, its main reason lies outside the body's essence. 
3. Natural motion. It represents the movement of the body itself. An example of this is the rising of the fire or the wind, the fall of the stone. This motion, in turn, moves in a form of circle and straight line. Motion is bound by space and time.
