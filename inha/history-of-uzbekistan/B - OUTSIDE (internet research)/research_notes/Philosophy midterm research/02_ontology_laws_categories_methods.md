## 1. Ontology: definition, etymology, being vs non-being, forms of being

### Takeaway
Ontology is the branch of philosophy that studies being ("what there is") and its most general features. The word is an early-modern coinage from Greek *on/ontos* ("being") + *logos* ("discourse, study"). It was first printed by Jacob Lorhard (1606) and Rudolf Goclenius (1613), and Christian Wolff systematised it in 1730 as "general metaphysics". Post-Soviet textbooks (following Frolov's *Введение в философию*, "Introduction to Philosophy") teach four basic forms of being: of things/nature, of the human, of the spiritual, and of the social. Analytic philosophy reframes the question in two ways: what a theory commits us to (Quine), and whether existence is a property at all (Kant, Frege–Russell, Meinong).

### Cited Findings

#### 1.1 Definition and etymology
- **Etymology.** The word combines Greek *on/ontos* ("being") and *logos* ("discourse") — [ontology.co: History of the term "ontology"](https://www.ontology.co/history.htm)
- **First printed use (1606).** Jacob Lorhard, *Ogdoas Scholastica* (St. Gallen, 1606). The title page reads "Metaphysices, seu Ontologiae", and the Latin term occurs four times in the work — [ontology.co](https://www.ontology.co/history.htm)
- **Goclenius (1613).** Rudolf Goclenius, *Lexicon philosophicum* (Frankfurt, 1613), entry *abstractio*: "οντολογία, philosophia de ente" ("ontology, the philosophy of being"). The word is written in Greek letters, and the use is casual rather than systematic — [ontology.co](https://www.ontology.co/history.htm)
- **Clauberg (1647).** Johannes Clauberg, *Elementa philosophiae sive Ontosophia* (Groningen, 1647), proposed "Ontosophia" or "Ontologia" as the science of being in general. He set it apart from theology, which deals specially with God — [ontology.co](https://www.ontology.co/history.htm)
- **Wolff (1730).** Christian Wolff, *Philosophia prima sive Ontologia* (1730), systematised the discipline. Ontology covers the general principles that apply to all entities (*metaphysica generalis*). *Metaphysica specialis* covers cosmology, psychology and theology — [ontology.co](https://www.ontology.co/history.htm)
- **Aristotle's "first philosophy"** is the ancestor of ontology. It is "the study of being qua being", i.e., of beings "as beings, in so far as they are beings". "Being" is said in many ways, all related to one central sense (*pros hen*), just as "healthy" applies to diets, exercise and people — [SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)
- Aristotle: "The beings in the primary sense are substances; the beings in other senses are the qualities, quantities, etc., that belong to substances" — [SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)
- **[WEST] Analytic definition.** Ontology is "the study of being, of what there is". *Meta-ontology* studies the nature and method of ontological inquiry, including how to tell what a theory is committed to — [SEP: Ontological Commitment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ontological-commitment/)

#### 1.2 Being and non-being
- **[SOV]** "Бытие в самом широком значении — существование" ("Being, in the broadest sense, is existence"). For a human being, it covers all activity: everyday actions, biological functions and mental processes, through interaction with the environment — [ru.wikipedia: Бытие](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5)
- The category goes back to **Parmenides**: "Бытие есть, небытия — вовсе нет" ("Being is; non-being is not at all"). Being is one, unchanging, eternal and indivisible, because admitting non-being would contradict this — [ru.wikipedia: Бытие](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5)
- **[UZ]** *Borliq* is "objektiv mavjud reallikni ifodalovchi falsafiy tushuncha" ("a philosophical concept expressing objectively existing reality"). It is broader than the material world of things. Related terms on the same page:
  - *vujudi mumkin* (possible/contingent existence) vs *vujudi vojib* (necessary existence), "from Islamic philosophy";
  - *real borliq* (with spatio-temporal properties) vs *xayoliy* (ideal) *borliq* (eternal, unchanging ideas and concepts);
  - *materiya* (matter), *harakat* (motion), *makon/fazo* (space), *zamon* (time).
  
  — [uz.wikipedia: Borliq](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borliq)
- **Hegel: Being → Nothing → Becoming.** Hegel's *Logic* opens with this triad.
  - Pure Being is "undetermined", so it "is really no different from ... pure negation"; "It is therefore equally a Nothing".
  - Becoming is the thought of their unity: "To Become is to go from Being to Nothing or from Nothing to Being".
  
  — [SEP: Hegel's Dialectics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)
- **[WEST] Kant.** Existence is not a real property: "not the least bit gets added to the thing when I posit in addition that this thing is". This was his objection to the ontological argument — [SEP: Existence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/existence/)
- **[WEST] Frege and Russell.** Existence is a second-order property, belonging to concepts or propositional functions, not to individuals: "to say that dinosaurs do not exist is to say that the property of being a dinosaur is not instantiated" — [SEP: Existence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/existence/)
- **[WEST] Meinong.** He separates being from existence. Objects such as the golden mountain or the round square have being without existing — [SEP: Existence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/existence/)
- **[WEST] Quine, "On What There Is" (1948).** "A theory is committed to those and only those entities to which the bound variables of the theory must be capable of referring in order that the affirmations made in the theory be true." This is summed up as "to be is to be the value of a variable" — [SEP: Ontological Commitment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ontological-commitment/)

#### 1.3 [SOV] The four basic forms of being (Frolov-type scheme)
ru.wikipedia attributes this classification to Frolov's *Введение в философию* — [ru.wikipedia: Бытие](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5)

| # | Form of being | Russian term | Sub-forms (per ru.wikipedia/Frolov) | Example (illustrative) |

| 3 | Being of the spiritual (ideal) | бытие духовного (идеального) | individualised spiritual (personal consciousness) vs objectified spiritual (culture existing outside individuals) | your memories and beliefs vs Navoi's poems, the Uzbek language, a library |
| 4 | Being of the social | бытие социального | individual being (a person within society and history) vs the being of society as a whole | your life in a family or mahalla vs Uzbek society as a whole |

  - *organik va noorganik tabiat* (organic and inorganic nature);
  - *biosfera* (biosphere);
  - *ijtimoiy borliq* (social being);
  - *obyektiv ideal borliq* (objective ideal being, e.g., cultural values and scientific principles);
  - *inson turmushi* (human life/existence).
  
  — [uz.wikipedia: Borliq](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borliq)

### Inferences
- **Exam one-liner:** "Ontology (Greek *ontos* 'being' + *logos* 'teaching') is the philosophical doctrine of being. The term dates from Lorhard (1606) and Goclenius (1613); Wolff (1730) systematised it as general metaphysics."
- **"Who coined it?" in an MCQ.**
  - If only Goclenius is offered (no Lorhard), pick Goclenius.
  - If both appear, ontology.co supports Lorhard (1606) as the earliest printed use.
  - Wolff popularised and systematised the term; he did not coin it.
- **Local link.** The uz.wikipedia pair *vujudi vojib / vujudi mumkin* matches Ibn Sina's (Avicenna's) "necessary vs possible existent". That is likely to come up in an Uzbek course. The attribution to Ibn Sina is general knowledge, not verified this session (see Gaps).
- **Worked example (illustrative): classify each item into a form of being.**
  - A wild tulip in the Chatkal mountains → being of things (first nature).
  - The Tashkent metro → being of things (second nature).
  - Your fear before an exam → spiritual being, individualised.
  - The *Avesta* or *Boburnoma* as texts → spiritual being, objectified.
  - Your mahalla's customs and the national economy → social being.
  - You, getting hungry and also voting → human being (body + social person).

## 2. Substance and matter; monism, dualism, pluralism; materialism vs idealism (objective vs subjective)

### Takeaway
Substance is the ultimate, self-standing bearer of all properties. Asking how many substances there are gives monism (one), dualism (two) or pluralism (many). Asking what the primary substance is gives materialism (matter/nature) or idealism (spirit/consciousness). That is the first side of what Engels called "the great basic question of all philosophy", the relation of thinking to being; its second side is knowability, which agnosticism denies. Idealism splits into subjective idealism (Berkeley: *esse est percipi*) and objective/absolute idealism (a supra-individual mind or Idea, as in the German idealists).

### Cited Findings

#### 2.1 Substance
- **[SOV] Spirkin on substance.**
  - Substance is "the vehicle, the bearer of all properties and relationships of everything that exists", "the underlying, most general vehicle" that persists through all transformations.
  - All things are "the specific, concrete forms of its manifestation".
  - For dialectical materialism this substance is matter; the section is titled "Matter as the Substance of Everything That Exists".
  
  — [Spirkin, *Dialectical Materialism*, ch. 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)
- **[WEST] SEP on substance.**
  - Substances are "those things that best merit the title 'beings'".
  - For Aristotle, substances are "not predicable of or attributable to anything else".
  - *Primary* substances are individuals (e.g., Fido). *Secondary* substances are their kinds (e.g., dog).
  - In the *Metaphysics*, Aristotle analyses substances as composites of matter and form.
  
  — [SEP: Substance](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/substance/)
- **OpenStax.** Substance is "the basic reality of a thing" (Latin *substantia*). Aristotle's hylomorphism makes a substance "a composite of matter and form", where form is "the unchanging purpose or whatness informing each particular". The pre-Socratics (starting with Thales of Miletus) proposed water, air, fire and "Being" as the fundamental reality — [OpenStax, *Introduction to Philosophy* 6.1 "Substance"](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/6-1-substance)
- **Descartes.** A (created) substance is "that which needs for its existence only the concurrence of God", i.e., independence is the criterion. He posited two created substances, mind and body, which gave rise to the mind–body problem — [SEP: Substance](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/substance/)
- **Spinoza and Leibniz.** Spinoza: only one substance exists, God or Nature (monism). Leibniz: many substances (monads), each expressing the universe from its own perspective — [SEP: Substance](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/substance/)
- **Locke, Hume and today.** Locke called substance an unknowable substratum, "something I know not what". Hume rejected substance and reduced things to bundles of properties. The contemporary analytic debate is bundle theory vs substratum theory — [SEP: Substance](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/substance/)

#### 2.2 [SOV] Matter
- **Lenin's definition (quoted by Spirkin):** "matter is a philosophical category denoting the objective reality which is given to man by his sensations, and which is copied, photographed and reflected by our sensations, while existing independently of them" — [Spirkin ch. 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)
- **Spirkin:** "Matter is everything that surrounds us, that exists outside our consciousness, that does not depend on our consciousness, and that is or may be reflected directly or indirectly in consciousness" — [Spirkin ch. 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)
- **Attributes of matter:** "The most important are objective existence, structure, indestructibility, motion, space, time, reflection and information" — [Spirkin ch. 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)
- **Structural levels of matter:** submicro-elementary → nuclear → atomic → molecular → macroscopic → cosmic, then organic/biological and social levels — [Spirkin ch. 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)
- **Matter and consciousness as the root categories:** "The initial categories for the whole system are those of matter and consciousness. They provide the trunk from which all the various branches of the other categories stem" — [Spirkin ch. 2.1](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02.html)

#### 2.3 Monism / dualism / pluralism
- **OpenStax.** Monism is "the belief that the most discrete or fundamental reality (i.e., 'the really real') is singular". Pluralism "asserts that fundamental reality consists of many types of being" — [OpenStax 6.1](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/6-1-substance)
- **[SOV] Spirkin.**
  - Materialist monism finds the unity of the world in matter.
  - Idealist monism finds it in a spiritual principle.
  - Dualism recognises "two parallel but independent worlds, the world of the spirit and the world of matter".
  
  — [Spirkin ch. 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)

#### 2.4 The basic question of philosophy: materialism vs idealism, knowability vs agnosticism
- Engels, *Ludwig Feuerbach and the End of Classical German Philosophy* (1886), part II: "The great basic question of all philosophy, especially of more recent philosophy, is that concerning the relation of thinking and being" — [Engels, Ludwig Feuerbach, II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)
- **Side 1, primacy.** "Did God create the world or has the world been in existence eternally?" Engels: "Those who asserted the primacy of spirit to nature ... comprised the camp of idealism. The others, who regarded nature as primary, belong to the various schools of materialism" — [same](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)
- **Side 2, knowability.** "In what relation do our thoughts about the world surrounding us stand to this world itself?" Against the agnostics (Hume, Kant): "The most telling refutation of this as of all other philosophical crotchets is practice — namely, experiment and industry". His example is alizarin dye, which chemists came to make from coal tar instead of extracting it from plants — [same](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)
- "These two expressions, idealism and materialism, originally signify nothing else but this" — [same](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)

#### 2.5 Kinds of idealism
- **[WEST] SEP's two senses of idealism.**
  - *Ontological (metaphysical) idealism*: "something mental (the mind, spirit, reason, will) is the ultimate foundation of all reality, or even exhaustive of reality".
  - *Epistemological idealism*: our knowledge of mind-independent reality is so permeated by the mind's constructive activity that knowledge claims are "a form of self-knowledge".
  
  — [SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)
- **Subjective idealism, Berkeley:** "esse is percipi" (to be is to be perceived); "an idea can be like nothing but an idea" — [SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)
- **Absolute/objective idealism (the later German idealists):** a universal mind or absolute that goes beyond individual consciousness grounds reality — [SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)
- **Kant's transcendental idealism:** space and time are "features of our own minds", not determinations of things in themselves. Kant combines this with "empirical realism" about external objects — [SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)
- Idealism's meaning "is often fixed by what is taken to be its opposite", whether that is materialism, realism or dogmatism — [SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)
- uz.wikipedia notes that interpretations of being range from "Plato's realm of forms to Hegel's dialectical idealism to materialist conceptions" — [uz.wikipedia: Borliq](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borliq)

### Inferences
- **Two different axes.** Monism/dualism/pluralism answers "how many?"; materialism/idealism answers "what kind?". Monism is therefore not the same as materialism: Hegel is an idealist monist and Marx a materialist monist. Spirkin explicitly distinguishes materialist from idealist monism.
- **Test for objective vs subjective idealism: whose mind is primary?**
  - A supra-individual Idea, World Spirit or Absolute → objective idealism (Hegel). Post-Soviet textbooks conventionally put Plato here too, a placement supported only indirectly (uz.wikipedia mentions "Plato's realm of forms").
  - The individual perceiver's sensations → subjective idealism (Berkeley).
- **Agnosticism is not idealism.** Agnosticism answers the *second* side of the basic question (knowability), not the first (primacy).
- **Lenin's definition of matter is epistemological, not physical.** It defines matter by its relation to consciousness: objective reality, given in sensation, existing independently of it. It never says "matter = atoms" or "matter = mass", which is why the definition survives changes in physics. The terminology differs between languages: Russian and Uzbek textbooks use *субстанция / substansiya* for the philosophical concept, but *вещество / modda* for "substance" in the physics/chemistry sense.
- **Worked example (illustrative): classify each claim.**
  - "The world is my perception" → subjective idealism.
  - "Nature is the externalisation of the Absolute Idea" → objective idealism (Hegel).
  - "Thought is a product of the brain; matter is primary" → materialism.
  - "Mind and body are two independent substances" → dualism (Descartes).
  - "God or Nature is the only substance" → monism (Spinoza).
  - "Reality consists of countless simple substances (monads)" → pluralism (Leibniz).
  - "We cannot know things as they are in themselves" → agnosticism (Kant, per Engels).
- **Commonly confused pairs (Theme 7, part 2)**

| Often confused | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Monism vs materialism | Monism = one substance (it may be spirit, as in Hegel). Materialism = matter is primary |
| Dualism vs dialectics | Dualism = two independent substances. Dialectics = the unity *and* struggle of opposites inside one developing whole |
| Objective vs subjective idealism | Supra-individual spirit/Idea vs the individual subject's perceptions |
| Idealism vs agnosticism | Primacy question vs knowability question |
| Substance vs matter | Substance = the formal role of "ultimate bearer of properties". In diamat, *matter* fills that role. For Spinoza it is God or Nature; for Descartes there are two such bearers |
| Primary vs secondary substance (Aristotle) | The individual (this horse) vs the kind (horse, animal) |
| Kant's transcendental idealism vs Berkeley | Kant keeps "empirical realism" about external objects. Berkeley's *esse est percipi* denies matter outside perception |

## 3. Motion, space and time (substantial vs relational conceptions)

### Takeaway
**[SOV]** Motion is the mode of existence of matter: every change "from mere change of place right up to thinking" (Engels). Motion is uncreatable and indestructible, while rest is only relative. Space and time are universal forms of the existence of matter: space is the order of coexistence, time the order of succession. The **substantial (absolute)** conception treats space and time as independent containers (Newton). The **relational** conception treats them as orders of relations among things and events (Leibniz, later supported by relativity). Diamat sides with the relational view while insisting that space and time are objective.

### Cited Findings

#### 3.1 Motion
- **Engels:** "Motion in the most general sense, conceived as the mode of existence, the inherent attribute of matter, comprehends all changes and processes occurring in the universe, from mere change of place right up to thinking" — [Engels, *Dialectics of Nature*, ch. III "Basic Forms of Motion"](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch03.htm)
- **Engels:** "motion also is as uncreatable as indestructible" — [DoN ch. III](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch03.htm)
- **Engels on forms of motion turning into each other:** "Mechanical motion of masses passes into heat, into electricity, into magnetism; heat and electricity pass into chemical decomposition; chemical combination in turn develops heat and electricity and, by means of the latter, magnetism; and finally, heat and electricity produce once more mechanical movement of masses" — [DoN ch. III](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch03.htm)
- **Spirkin on motion:** "Motion is the mode of existence of matter. To be means to be in motion." Motion is self-motion: "the tendency, the impulse to change of state is inherent in matter itself" — [Spirkin ch. 2.3](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s03.html)
- **Spirkin on rest:** "Possessing absolute rest means ceasing to exist." "Bodies may rest only in relation to a given system of reference, conventionally regarded as motionless" — [Spirkin ch. 2.3](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s03.html)
- **Spirkin's forms of motion:** elementary-particle, atomic/molecular, thermal, mechanical, biological, social (and conceivably "metasocial") forms. Each has its own material carrier. Higher forms include lower ones but keep their own distinctive character — [Spirkin ch. 2.3](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s03.html)

#### 3.2 Space and time
- **Definitions.** Space is "a form of coordination of coexisting objects and states of matter". Time is "a form of coordination of objects and states of matter in their succession". Both are "universal forms of the existence of matter" — [Spirkin ch. 2.4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s04.html)
- **Properties.** Space has "three dimensions: length, breadth and height". Time has only one dimension and flows "from the past through the present to the future. It is inevitable, unrepeatable and irreversible" — [Spirkin ch. 2.4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s04.html)
- **Newtonian vs relativistic picture.** In the Newtonian picture, space was "the empty container of things" and time "flowed by in the same way throughout the universe". Relativity theory showed instead that "with the disappearance of matter space and time would also disappear" — [Spirkin ch. 2.4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s04.html)
- **[WEST] The two classical positions.**
  - *Absolutism (substantivalism)*: space and time are independent entities in their own right.
  - *Relationism*: space is "an order of coexistence" and time "an order of succession" among things, not independent substances.
  
  — [SEP: Absolute and Relational Space and Motion: Classical Theories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories/)
- **Newton.** The *Scholium* to the *Principia* defends absolute space and time. In the bucket experiment, the water's rotation relative to the vessel produces no noticeable centrifugal effects; such forces appear with rotation relative to the earth and the other celestial bodies, which Newton took as evidence of absolute rotation. In the rotating-globes thought experiment, two globes joined by a cord could detect their rotation from the cord's tension "without needing any reference to anything external" — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories/)
- **Leibniz.** He used the Principle of Sufficient Reason and the Identity of Indiscernibles in the "shifted universe" argument: a universe shifted in absolute space would be indistinguishable from this one, so absolute space is unnecessary — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories/)
- **Mach (1883)** mounted "the most sustained, comprehensive, and influential attack on absolute space" — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories/)

#### 3.3 Comparison table

| | Substantial (absolute, substantivalist) conception | Relational conception |
|---|---|---|
| Core claim | Space and time are independent entities, "containers" | Space and time are orders of relations among things and events (coexistence, succession) |
| Key names | Newton (*Principia* Scholium; bucket, globes) | Leibniz (Leibniz–Clarke correspondence); Mach; relativity |
| If all matter vanished... | empty space and time would remain | space and time would vanish with it (Spirkin, on relativity) |
| Diamat stance | rejected | adopted, plus the claim that space and time are *objective* forms of matter |
| Sources | [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories/); [Spirkin 2.4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s04.html) | same |

### Inferences
- **Diamat's definitions are Leibniz's formulas made materialist.** Spirkin's "coordination of coexisting objects" (space) and "in their succession" (time) almost restate Leibniz's "order of coexistence / order of succession", but say these orders are objective features of matter.
- **Three positions that make good MCQ distractors.**
  - (a) Substantial/absolute: Newton.
  - (b) Relational: Leibniz, adopted by diamat with the relativity backing.
  - (c) Subjective/transcendental: Kant, for whom space and time are forms of *our* sensibility ([SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)).
- **Worked example (illustrative): relative rest.** A student sits "at rest" in a Tashkent metro carriage. She is at rest relative to the carriage, but moving relative to the platform, the rotating Earth and the Sun. Rest is relative to a chosen frame, motion is never absent. Inside her body, physical, chemical and biological motion run at once, and her thinking is the highest form of motion: it includes the lower forms but cannot be reduced to them.
- **Commonly confused pairs**

| Often confused | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Motion vs mechanical movement | Motion = *any* change (including chemical, biological, social change and thinking). Mechanical movement = change of place only |
| Relational vs "relative/relativism" | "Relational" is a thesis about what space and time *are* (orders of relations). It does not mean space and time are subjective or "all relative" |
| Substantial conception vs substance (§2) | Same root word, different topics: space as an independent container vs the ultimate bearer of properties |
| Properties of time vs space | Time: one-dimensional, irreversible, unrepeatable. Space: three-dimensional, and you can return to a place but not to a moment |
| Kant vs diamat on space/time | Kant: forms of the mind. Diamat: objective forms of the existence of matter |

III describes mechanical, physical (heat, electricity, magnetism) and chemical motion and their interconversion, while Spirkin gives a longer list that includes biological and social motion. Check the slide's version.

## 4. Parmenides vs Heraclitus: the classic contrast on being

### Takeaway
**Parmenides of Elea** (flourished early 5th century BCE) held that "what is" is ungenerated, imperishable, whole, unchanging and one. Change and plurality are not truly real, and reason, not the senses, reveals being. This makes him the founder of ontology and the classic "static" view. **Heraclitus of Ephesus** (c. 500 BCE) held that everything changes within a stable order (the Logos). For him opposites form a unity, and the cosmos is an "everliving fire, kindling in measures and being quenched in measures". The dialectical tradition treats him as its ancient forerunner.

### Cited Findings

#### 4.1 Parmenides
- **Life and work.** Parmenides of Elea, a Greek colony on the southern coast of Italy, flourished in the early 5th century BCE (a date around 515 BCE is inferred from Plato's dialogues). His single work was a hexameter poem, of which roughly 160 verses survive. It has three parts: a **proem** (a journey to a goddess), the **Way of Truth** (*aletheia*) and the **Way of Opinion** (*doxa*, a cosmology) — [SEP: Parmenides](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parmenides/)
- **Two ways of inquiry:** "that [it] is and that [it] is not possible for it not to be" vs "that it is not and that it must not be" — [SEP: Parmenides](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parmenides/)
- **Fragment 8.** What is must be "ungenerated and deathless, / whole and uniform, and still and perfect". It cannot change, move or vary internally, and Parmenides conceives it as spherical — [SEP: Parmenides](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parmenides/)
- **Reality vs appearance.** He denies that genuine change and plurality are real and contrasts unchanging Being with the deceptive world of the senses. His arguments shaped Zeno, Melissus, Plato and the atomists. Scholars still debate whether he was a strict monist, a logical analyst, or a metaphysician distinguishing necessary from contingent being — [SEP: Parmenides](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parmenides/) (entry by John Palmer; first published 2008, revised 4 March 2025)
- **The Russian textbook slogan:** "Бытие есть, небытия — вовсе нет" ("Being is; non-being is not at all") — [ru.wikipedia: Бытие](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5)

#### 4.2 Heraclitus
- **Life and doctrines.** Heraclitus of Ephesus in Ionia, c. 500 BCE, wrote in oracular, riddling language. His themes were universal change, opposing forces, and fire as the basic reality — [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/)
- **River fragment B12:** "On those stepping into rivers staying the same other and other waters flow". This is the most authentic of the river fragments: the river keeps its identity while its waters constantly change — [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/)
- **Flux.** Things undergo constant transformation, "though not in the destructive sense Plato attributed to him" — [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/)
- **Unity of opposites (B88):** "living and dead, waking and sleeping, young and old" are the same in us. Contraries are bound together, not merely logically contradictory — [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/)
- **Fire (B30).** The world-order is an "everliving fire, kindling in measures and being quenched in measures" — [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/)
- **Logos.** Understanding the universal Logos shows that "all things are one", though most people remain oblivious to it — [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/)
- **[UZ]** uz.wikipedia's *Dialektika* article presents Heraclitus' view of dialectics as "doimiy vujudga kelish, rivojlanish hamda tanazzulga yuz tutish" ("continuous coming-into-being, development and decline"), driven by contradictions — [uz.wikipedia: Dialektika](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialektika)

#### 4.3 Comparison table

| | Parmenides | Heraclitus |
|---|---|---|
| City / date | Elea (southern Italy), early 5th c. BCE | Ephesus (Ionia), c. 500 BCE |
| Being | One, ungenerated, imperishable, whole, motionless, "like" a sphere | An ever-changing process held together by the Logos |
| Change | Not genuinely real (an appearance, the "way of opinion") | Constant; identity persists *through* change (river B12) |
| Opposites | Non-being is excluded entirely | Opposites form a unity (B88) |
| Basic principle | "It is" (being) | Fire, "kindling in measures" (B30) |
| Path to truth | Reason (the Way of Truth) over the senses | Grasping the Logos, which most people miss |
| Legacy | Zeno, Melissus, Plato, the atomists; the start of ontology | Forerunner of dialectics (uz.wikipedia) |
| Sources | [SEP Parmenides](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parmenides/) | [SEP Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/); [uz.wikipedia](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialektika) |

### Inferences
- **Exam formula:** Parmenides: "Being is, non-being is not" → no real change. Heraclitus: "everything flows" (identity in change, unity of opposites) → change is basic.
- **Hegel's opening can be read as reconciling the two** (my interpretation, not a cited claim). The *Logic* starts from Parmenidean pure Being, shows that it is "equally a Nothing", and makes Heraclitean Becoming the truth of both.
- **Zeno and Engels.** Zeno's paradoxes defended Parmenides against motion (SEP lists Zeno among those Parmenides influenced). Engels' dialectical reply, "Motion itself is a contradiction ... a body being at one and the same moment of time both in one place and in another place" ([Anti-Dühring, ch. XII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch10.htm)), is the classic counterpoint.
- **"Measure" connects Heraclitus to Theme 8.** Heraclitus' fire is kindled "in measures", and the dialectical category of measure (§5) echoes this.
- **Commonly confused pairs**

| Often confused | Key difference |
|---|---|
| "You cannot step into the same river twice" vs B12 | The famous one-liner is a later paraphrase. SEP treats B12 ("other and other waters flow" on those stepping into the *same* rivers) as authentic, and it stresses identity *through* change, not chaos |
| Heraclitus' fire vs Thales' water | Both are candidates for the basic stuff ([OpenStax 6.1](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/6-1-substance)). Heraclitus' fire also stands for ordered change ("in measures") |
| Eleatics vs Ionians | Elea (Parmenides, Zeno): being and permanence. Ionia/Miletus/Ephesus (Thales, Heraclitus): basic stuff and change |
| Parmenides' "sphere" | A comparison for completeness and uniformity, not a physical ball in space |

### Gaps
- I did not verify Heraclitus fragments B53 ("war is father of all"), B60 ("the road up and down is one and the same") or B91 ("cannot step twice into the same river") in a fetched source. Use them with care.
- The post-Soviet label for Heraclitus as founder of "spontaneous (naive) dialectics" was not found verbatim. uz.wikipedia only associates him with dialectics.

## 5. The three laws of dialectics (Hegel → Engels → post-Soviet textbooks)

### Takeaway
Engels named three laws that Hegel had developed "in his idealist fashion as mere laws of *thought*":
1. the transformation of quantity into quality and vice versa (from Hegel's Doctrine of Being);
2. the interpenetration of opposites, later taught as the "unity and struggle of opposites" (from the Doctrine of Essence);
3. the negation of the negation (the construction principle of Hegel's whole system).

Post-Soviet teaching pairs each law with one question about development:
- unity and struggle of opposites → the **source** (why: internal contradiction, self-movement);
- quantity into quality → the **mechanism** (how: gradual quantitative accumulation, breach of the measure, a leap);
- negation of the negation → the **direction/form** (where to: progressive, spiral-like, with a partial return on a higher level).

### Cited Findings

#### 5.1 Engels' formulation and its Hegelian roots
- **The three laws:** "The law of the transformation of quantity into quality and *vice versa*; The law of the interpenetration of opposites; The law of the negation of the negation" — [Engels, *Dialectics of Nature*, ch. II "Dialectics"](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch02.htm)
- **Where each comes from in Hegel:** "All three are developed by Hegel in his idealist fashion as mere laws of *thought*: the first, in the first part of his *Logic*, in the *Doctrine of Being*; the second fills the whole of the second and by far the most important part of his *Logic*, the *Doctrine of Essence*; finally the third figures as the fundamental law for the construction of the whole system" — [DoN ch. II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch02.htm)
- **Engels' criticism of Hegel and the materialist fix.** In Hegel the laws "are foisted on nature and history as laws of thought, and not deduced from them". Engels' remedy: "If we turn the thing round, then everything becomes simple, and the dialectical laws that look so extremely mysterious in idealist philosophy at once become simple and clear as noonday" — [DoN ch. II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch02.htm)
- **Engels' definition of dialectics:** "nothing more than the science of the general laws of motion and development of nature, human society and thought" — [Engels, *Anti-Dühring* (1877), Part I ch. XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm)
- **Dates.** *Dialectics of Nature* was written 1873–1886 and first published "in Russian and German in the USSR in 1925" — [DoN index](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/index.htm)
- **Hegel's merit and limit, per Engels.** In Hegel's system, "for the first time the whole world ... is represented as a process — i.e., as in constant motion, change, transformation, development". But Hegel was an idealist, so for him things were "realized pictures of the 'Idea'" — [Engels, *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific*, ch. II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm)
- **Hegel's own three "moments" of the logical (Encyclopaedia Logic §§79–82).**
  - (1) **Understanding** (the abstract side): "the moment of fixity, in which concepts or forms have a seemingly stable definition or determination".
  - (2) **Dialectical, or negatively rational**: "the moment of instability", in which a one-sided determination "passes into its opposite".
  - (3) **Speculative, or positively rational**: it "grasps the unity of the opposition between the first two determinations, or is the positive result of the dissolution".
  
  — [SEP: Hegel's Dialectics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)
- **Self-movement in Hegel.** The movement to new determinations "comes about on its own accord", and "the dialectical constitutes the moving soul of scientific progression" (EL Remark to §81) — [SEP: Hegel's Dialectics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)
- **Aufheben (sublation)** has "a doubled meaning: it means both to cancel (or negate) and to preserve at the same time". **Determinate negation**: "Because the result, the negation, is a *determinate* negation, it has a *content*" — [SEP: Hegel's Dialectics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)
- **Thesis–antithesis–synthesis.** The SEP entry notes that Hegel did not adopt Fichte's triadic "thesis–antithesis–synthesis" language for his own method, although interpreters map Being/Nothing/Becoming onto it. It cites Mueller (1958) as the classic criticism of that reading — [SEP: Hegel's Dialectics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/) (first published 2016, revised 2020)

  - the same drug stimulates or inhibits depending on dose.
  
  — [Spirkin 2.9](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s09.html)
- **ru.wikipedia's examples:** water's phase transitions, the threshold of intoxication, children outgrowing clothes, the periodic table (adding protons) — [ru.wikipedia](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B0_%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D1%8B%D1%85_%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%B2_%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D1%8B%D0%B5)

#### 5.3 Law 2: unity and struggle (interpenetration) of opposites
- **Opposite.** An opposite is "one of the mutually presupposed sides of a contradiction", and "the extreme case of difference is an opposite". "Identity and difference have their degrees. Difference, for example, can be inessential or essential" — [Spirkin 2.11](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s11.html)
- **Dialectical contradiction** is "the unity of opposites and their struggle". "Opposites may come into conflict only to the extent that they form a whole in which one element is as necessary as another". "The interaction of opposites, as a contradiction and its resolution, is what awakens every seed to growth" — [Spirkin 2.11](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s11.html)
- **Types of contradiction.**
  - "Internal contradictions are interaction of opposite sides within a given system".
  - "External contradictions are the interaction of opposites related to different systems".
  - "Antagonistic contradictions are interactions between implacably hostile classes".
  - "Non-antagonistic contradictions are interactions between classes whose basic interests coincide".
  
  Harmony is a "balanced and viable stable combination of elements and their connections" — [Spirkin 2.11](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s11.html)
- **Lenin on the essence of dialectics:** "The splitting of a single whole and the cognition of its contradictory parts ... is the essence of dialectics" — [Lenin, "On the Question of Dialectics" (1915)](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm)
- **Lenin's examples of opposites:**
  - mathematics: + and −, differential and integral;
  - mechanics: action and reaction;
  - physics: positive and negative electricity;
  - chemistry: the combination and dissociation of atoms;
  - social science: the class struggle.
  
  — [Lenin 1915](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm)
- **Lenin on development.** "Development is the 'struggle' of opposites." Only the conception of development as a unity of opposites furnishes the key to "self-movement", to "leaps", to the "break in continuity" and to the "transformation into the opposite" — [Lenin 1915](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm)
- **Lenin on unity vs struggle:** "The unity ... of opposites is conditional, temporary, transitory, relative. The struggle of mutually exclusive opposites is absolute" — [Lenin 1915](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm) (written 1915 in Bern; first published 1925 in *Bolshevik*)
- **Engels on motion as contradiction:** "Motion itself is a contradiction: even simple mechanical change of position can only come about through a body being at one and the same moment of time both in one place and in another place" — [Anti-Dühring ch. XII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch10.htm)
- **Stalin's version (1938):** "Internal contradictions are inherent in all things and phenomena of nature ... the struggle between these opposites ... constitutes the internal content of the process of development" — [Stalin, "Dialectical and Historical Materialism" (1938)](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm)
- **ru.wikipedia on the law.** Contradictions are objective and are internal sources of development. Opposites are sides that "always exclude each other, complement each other, and exist in inseparable unity". Examples: light/darkness, good/evil, wave–particle duality, heredity/variability in evolution. From Engels' perspective, the basis of all development is the struggle of opposite sides — [ru.wikipedia: Закон единства и борьбы противоположностей](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B0_%D0%B8_%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%8C%D0%B1%D1%8B_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%B9)

#### 5.4 Law 3: negation of the negation
- **Engels on dialectical negation:** "Negation in dialectics does not mean simply saying no, or declaring that something does not exist, or destroying it in any way one likes." He cites Spinoza: "Omnis determinatio est negatio — every limitation or determination is at the same time a negation." And: "I must not only negate, but also sublate the negation. I must therefore so arrange the first negation that the second remains or becomes possible." Grinding a grain of barley or crushing an insect is a negation that makes the second step impossible — [Anti-Dühring ch. XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm)
- **The barley grain:** "the grain as such ceases to exist, it is negated, and in its place appears the plant which has arisen from it ... As a result of this negation of the negation we have once again the original grain of barley, but not as a single unit, but ten-, twenty- or thirtyfold" — [Anti-Dühring ch. XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm)
- **Engels' other examples:**
  - butterflies "spring from the egg by a negation of the egg";
  - algebra: *a* → −*a* → +*a*², "the original positive quantity, but at a higher degree";
  - philosophy: "The old materialism was therefore negated by idealism", and idealism in turn was negated by modern materialism;
  - property: common ownership of land → private property → common property, "far higher and more developed".
  
  — [Anti-Dühring ch. XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm)
- **Engels' definition:** "And so, what is the negation of the negation? An extremely general — and for this reason extremely far-reaching and important — law of development of nature, history, and thought" — [Anti-Dühring ch. XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm)
- **Spirkin on the two kinds of negation.** "Dialectical negation is primarily a creative and conserving negation. The old is not simply thrown aside but is 'subsumed', preserved in the new." Destructive negation, by contrast, "pushes the past into the abyss of 'nothingness'" — [Spirkin 2.10](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s10.html)
- **Spirkin's three stages and the spiral.** The stages are: "the initial moment of development; the transformation of phenomena into their opposites, that is to say, negation; the transformation of the new opposite into its opposite, that is to say, the negation of negation". "Development is not a straight line and not motion in a circle, but a spiral with an infinite series of turns". It involves "a kind of return to previous stages, when certain features of obsolete and replaced forms are repeated in new forms" — [Spirkin 2.10](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s10.html)
- **Spirkin on development:** it "is characterised by continuity, consistency, direction, irreversibility and the preservation of achieved results" — [Spirkin 2.10](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s10.html)
- **ru.wikipedia on the third law.** "Третий закон диалектики отражает цикличность процесса развития и его направленность ... восходящий тренд формирует спиралевидную форму" ("The third law of dialectics reflects the cyclical character of development and its *directionality* ... the ascending trend forms a spiral"). Negation of the negation means that the old quality is overcome while what was accumulated at the earlier stage is taken up again in a new form. The article also mentions Ilyenkov's critique of treating dialectical formulas as a-priori schemes — [ru.wikipedia: Закон отрицания отрицания](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F_%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F)
- **Stalin's omission.** Stalin's 1938 list of the "principal features" of the Marxist dialectical method does not mention the negation of the negation at all — [Stalin 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm)

### Inferences
- **Why source / mechanism / direction?** This mapping is a teaching synthesis that the sources support piece by piece.
  - *Source*: contradiction is the "internal source", the "key to self-movement" (ru.wikipedia; Lenin).
  - *Mechanism*: development "takes place through the accumulation of quantitative changes ... leap" (ru.wikipedia), and the path is a "zigzag" (Spirkin).
  - *Direction*: negation of the negation reflects "directionality" and a "spiral" (ru.wikipedia; Spirkin).
  
  Mnemonic: **WHY – HOW – WHERE** = **Opposites – Quantity/Quality – Negation**.
- **Order of the laws.** Engels lists quantity → quality first, opposites second, negation third (DoN). Many later textbooks put "unity and struggle of opposites" first, following Lenin's view that it is the "essence of dialectics". Check which order your slides use. Negation of the negation is third in both orders.
- **Worked example 1: Law 1, step by step (water; Engels).**
  1. *Quality*: liquid water.
  2. *Quantitative change within the measure*: heating from 20 °C to 99 °C at normal pressure. The temperature changes, but it is still water. The measure is the 0–100 °C interval.
  3. *Nodal point → leap*: at 100 °C, water becomes steam (at 0 °C, ice).
  4. *"And vice versa"* (illustrative): the new quality brings a new measure and new quantitative characteristics. Steam can be heated far above 100 °C and occupies a far larger volume.
  5. *Mechanism*: development = gradual accumulation + a leap when the measure is exceeded.
  
  Everyday illustration: the Uzbek proverb *"Tomchi-tomchi ko'l bo'lur"* ("drop by drop, a lake forms"). Another: vocabulary studied day by day until you suddenly "speak" the language, which is a leap of the gradual type.
- **Worked example 2: Law 2, step by step (heredity vs variability; ru.wikipedia).**
  1. *Unity*: a species exists only as the unity of heredity, which conserves traits, and variability, which produces new ones. Each is "as necessary as another" (Spirkin).
  2. *Struggle*: they pull in opposite directions, toward stability and toward change.
  3. *Resolution*: selection fixes useful variants → a new form, which carries a new contradiction.
  4. *Source*: the impulse is internal (self-movement), not an outside push.
  
  Student illustration (illustrative): what you already know vs a problem you cannot yet solve. The tension drives learning, and resolving it creates a new level with new problems.
- **Worked example 3: Law 3, step by step (barley; Engels).**
  1. *Initial state*: a grain.
  2. *First negation*: the plant (the grain "ceases to exist").
  3. *Negation of the negation*: new grains, "ten-, twenty- or thirtyfold". This is a return to the grain at a higher level.
  4. *Not* dialectical: grinding the grain into flour, which destroys the possibility of the second negation.
  5. *Direction*: an ascending spiral, neither a straight line nor a circle.
  
  Modern illustration (illustrative): handwritten letters → telephone (writing negated by voice) → text messages and email (writing returns, but instant and global).
- **Analytic contrast (my gloss, not verified in a fetched source).** Analytic logicians reserve "contradiction" for asserting both A and not-A. They therefore read diamat's "contradictions" as real oppositions, tensions or conflicts of forces, not as logical inconsistencies. This is the core of Popper-style criticism.
- **Commonly confused pairs (laws)**

| Often confused | Key difference | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Quality vs property | A property is how *one aspect* of a quality shows itself in relation to other objects. Quality is the integral definiteness of the whole thing | Spirkin 2.9 |
| Quantity vs measure | Quantity is the external, formal side (number, size, degree). Measure is the quantitative *limit* within which a given quality persists | Spirkin 2.9 |
| A leap is always an explosion? | No. Leaps can be momentary *or* gradual ("a process of a certain duration") | Spirkin 2.9 |
| Difference vs opposite | "The extreme case of difference is an opposite" | Spirkin 2.11 |
| Unity vs struggle | Unity is "relative" and temporary. Struggle is "absolute" | Lenin 1915 |
| Internal vs external contradiction | Within one system vs between systems | Spirkin 2.11 |
| Antagonistic vs non-antagonistic contradiction | Between hostile classes vs between groups whose basic interests coincide | Spirkin 2.11 |
| Dialectical vs formal-logical contradiction | The first is an objective unity and struggle of opposite sides within a whole. The second is an error of reasoning (affirming and denying the same thing in the same respect; my gloss) | Spirkin 2.11 + inference |
| Dialectical vs metaphysical (destructive) negation | Dialectical negation preserves and sublates. Destructive negation throws the past into "the abyss of 'nothingness'" (e.g., grinding the barley) | Spirkin 2.10; Engels AD XIII |
| Spiral vs circle vs line | Development is "not a straight line and not motion in a circle, but a spiral" | Spirkin 2.10 |
| "Thesis–antithesis–synthesis" = Hegel? | Fichte's language, which Hegel did not adopt for his own method | SEP |
| Hegel's laws vs Engels' laws | Same content. Hegel's are laws of *thought* (idealist). Engels "turns them round" into laws of nature, history and thought | DoN II |

## 6. Categories of dialectics (paired categories) vs Aristotle's 10 and Kant's 12

### Takeaway
Spirkin defines categories as "extremely general, fundamental concepts reflecting the most essential, law-governed connections and relationships of reality". Diamat teaches them as correlative pairs or triads, and each side exists only in relation to the other:
- singular–particular–universal;
- essence–phenomenon;
- content–form;
- cause–effect;
- necessity–chance;
- possibility–reality.

For contrast:
- Aristotle's 10 categories are the highest kinds of being, found by asking different questions about a thing.
- Kant's 12 categories are a-priori concepts of the understanding, derived from the forms of judgment.
- Analytic philosophy asks whether categories describe reality itself (category realism) or only our conceptual scheme (descriptivism).

### Cited Findings

#### 6.1 What categories are
- **[SOV] Spirkin.** "Categories are extremely general, fundamental concepts reflecting the most essential, law-governed connections and relationships of reality." They are also "the forms and stable organising principles of the thought process and, as such, they reproduce the properties and relations of existence in global and most concentrated form" — [Spirkin ch. 2.1 "The Categories of Dialectics"](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02.html)
- **[WEST] SEP.** "A system of categories is a complete list of highest kinds or genera."
  - *Category realism*: categories are the "highest genera of entities", providing "an inventory of everything there is".
  - *Descriptivism*: categories describe the categorial structure the world would have "according to our thought, experience, or language", without committing to whether the categories are occupied.
  
  — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/) (Amie Thomasson; revised 22 Nov 2022)

| **Occasion** (pretext; Russian *повод*) | "the external push or circumstance that sets in motion a train of underlying interconnections" | a head cold that triggers an underlying illness | [Spirkin 2.6](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s06.html) |
| **Necessity** | "the impossibility of something being otherwise" | Engels: someone like Napoleon was historically necessary | [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html); [Engels 1894](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894/letters/94_01_25.htm) |
| **Chance** (accident, contingency) | "expresses mainly external, contingent, inessential events"; it is "a form of manifestation of necessity itself" | slipping on a banana peel; a baby's sex (necessary for the embryo's development, accidental for history); a war that depends on the "mood of a monarch" or "offended vanity" | [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html) |
| **Possibility** | *real* possibility is a "law-governed tendency in development"; *formal* possibility lacks sufficient conditions and is "very remote"; "Possibility is a unity of existence and non existence" | formal: a satellite hitting a meteorite. A child's capacity for rational thought is a possibility not yet realised | [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html) |
| **Reality** (actuality) | realised possibility | space flight was "once only formal but has now become reality" | [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html) |

#### 6.3 Causality: further points
- **Interaction.** "Any effect is evoked by the interaction of at least two phenomena. Therefore the interaction phenomenon is the true cause" — [Spirkin 2.6](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s06.html)
- **Determinism** is the recognition that "a regular sequence of phenomena and conditions always takes the form of realisation of causal connections". It "involves the recognition of objective necessity, which in turn implies objective accidentality". Statistical laws are "one of the forms of manifestation of determinism", not a refutation of it — [Spirkin 2.6](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s06.html)
- **[WEST/classical] Aristotle's four causes:**
  - material ("bronze, silver ... causes of a statue");
  - formal ("the form ... the account of the essence");
  - efficient ("the primary source of change or rest");
  - final ("the end, that for which a thing is done").
  
  "Form, mover, and *telos* often coincide" — [SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)

#### 6.4 Necessity and chance
- **Spirkin:** "The necessary carves a road for itself through an infinite number of accidents". "Both in nature and society, where chance appears to dominate, it is in reality subordinate to certain laws. But not everything that happens does so of necessity" — [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html)
- **Engels:** societies "are governed by necessity, which is supplemented by and appears under the forms of accident" — [Engels to W. Borgius, London, 25 January 1894](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894/letters/94_01_25.htm) (formerly misattributed as a letter to H. Starkenburg)
- **Engels' Napoleon example:** "That Napoleon, just that particular Corsican, should have been the military dictator whom the French Republic, exhausted by its own war, had rendered necessary, was an accident; but that, if a Napoleon had been lacking, another would have filled the place, is proved by the fact that the man has always been found as soon as he became necessary: Caesar, Augustus, Cromwell, etc." — [Engels 1894](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894/letters/94_01_25.htm)

#### 6.5 Possibility and reality vs Aristotle's potentiality and actuality
- **[SOV] Spirkin:** the real vs formal distinction and the space-flight example (see the table) — [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html)
- **[Classical] Aristotle.** Potentiality (*dunamis*) is the capacity to be in a different state. Actuality (*energeia/entelecheia*) is the realised activity or completion. Examples: the builder with craft knowledge; the acorn becoming an oak; "what is awake is in relation to what is asleep". Actuality is prior, because "what is eternal must be fully actual" — [SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)

#### 6.6 Other pairs that often appear on exams
- **System and element.** A system is "an internally organised whole where elements are so intimately connected that they operate as one in relation to external conditions and other systems". An element is "the minimal unit performing a definite function in the whole" — [Spirkin 2.7](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s07.html)
- **Structure** is "the type of connection between the elements of a whole" — [Spirkin 2.7](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s07.html)
- **Part and whole.** "The categories of whole and part express a relationship between objects in which one object, being a complex and integral whole, is a unity of other objects which form its parts" — [Spirkin 2.7](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s07.html)

#### 6.7 Aristotle's ten categories (*Categories* 1b25)
| # | Category | Aristotle's own examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Substance | man, horse |
| 2 | Quantity | four-foot, five-foot |
| 3 | Quality | white, grammatical |
| 4 | Relation | double, half |
| 5 | Place (where) | in the Lyceum, in the marketplace |
| 6 | Date / time (when) | yesterday, last year |
| 7 | Posture (position) | is lying, is sitting |
| 8 | State (having) | has shoes on, has armour on |
| 9 | Action (doing) | cutting, burning |
| 10 | Passion (being affected) | being cut, being burned |

- **How Aristotle found them.** He distinguished "different questions which may be asked about something", noting "that only a limited range of answers can be appropriately given to any particular question" — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)
- **Primary vs secondary substances.** Primary substances (an individual man) come before secondary substances (the species *man*, the genus *animal*): "all the other things are either said of the primary substances as subjects or in them as subjects" — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)

#### 6.8 Kant's twelve categories (pure concepts of the understanding)
| Heading (moment of judgment) | The three categories |
|---|---|
| **Quantity** | Unity · Plurality · Totality |
| **Quality** | Reality · Negation · Limitation |
| **Relation** | Inherence and Subsistence (substance and accident) · Causality and Dependence (cause and effect) · Community (reciprocity) |
| **Modality** | Possibility · Existence · Necessity |

- **How Kant derived them.** Kant starts "from Aristotelian logic in outlining four respects in which one can classify any judgment: according to its quantity, quality, relation, or modality". The categories are the correlates of the forms of judgment, which guarantees that they apply to "all objects of possible cognition" — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)

#### 6.9 Later and analytic approaches [WEST]
- **Husserl** distinguished formal ontological categories (object, state of affairs, property, relation, number) from material categories or "regions" (nature, culture, consciousness) — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)
- **Ryle.** "Two expressions differ in category if there are contexts in which substituting one for the other results in absurdity". His example of mixing categories: "She came home in a flood of tears and a sedan-chair" — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)
- **Contemporary systems:**
  - Chisholm (1996): a tree with *Entia* at the top;
  - Hoffman & Rosenkrantz (1994): abstract vs concrete entities;
  - E. J. Lowe's four-category ontology (2006): objects, modes, kinds, attributes.
  
  — [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)

#### 6.10 Comparison of category systems

### Inferences
- **Worked examples for each pair (illustrative; they apply the cited definitions).**
  1. **Singular / particular / universal.**
     - *Tashkent* (singular: unique, has a proper name);
     - *the cities of Central Asia* (particular: a group in which "city" is realised in a specific form);
     - *city* (universal: "the singular in the many").
     
     There is no "city in general" apart from actual cities. The universal exists only in and through singulars, which is Lenin's point that "the individual is the universal".
  2. **Essence / phenomenon / semblance.**
     - A doctor sees fever and a cough (phenomena) and infers the infection (essence). Diagnosis moves from phenomenon to essence, as science does.
     - The Sun "rising and setting" is a *semblance*: the essence is the Earth's rotation.
  3. **Content / form.**
     - A course's knowledge and skills are its content; its organisation into lectures, seminars and online modules is its form.
     - An outdated form (e.g., pure dictation) can "lag behind" and slow learning, while a good form (interactive seminars) speeds it up.
     - Content is determining: change what students must learn and the form must eventually change too.
  4. **Cause / effect / condition / occasion.** A dormitory fire:
     - *cause* = a short circuit that generates the ignition;
     - *conditions* = oxygen and flammable materials (they cannot cause the fire, but the cause is powerless without them);
     - *occasion* = switching on an overloaded heater (the external push).
     
     The standard textbook history example: in WWI, imperialist rivalry is the cause and the Sarajevo assassination the occasion. This is a common illustration, not verified in a fetched source.
  5. **Necessity / chance.**
     - Each toss of a fair coin is a matter of chance, but about 50% heads over thousands of tosses is a statistical regularity, "one of the forms of manifestation of determinism" (Spirkin).
     - Napoleon the person was an accident. A military dictator in post-revolutionary France was a necessity (Engels).
  6. **Possibility / reality.**
     - A first-year Inha student becoming an engineer is a *real* possibility: the law-governed conditions exist (curriculum, ability, time).
     - Winning a lottery jackpot is a *formal (abstract)* possibility: not ruled out, but the conditions are "very remote".
     - Graduation turns the real possibility into reality.
- **Mnemonics.**
  - Aristotle's 10 in order: **S**ubstance, **Q**uantity, **Q**uality, **R**elation, **P**lace, **T**ime, **P**osture, **S**tate, **A**ction, **P**assion. The four "accident" headings everyone remembers are quantity, quality, relation and place/time; the "small four" are posture, state, action, passion.
  - Kant's 12: "**QQRM**" (Quantity, Quality, Relation, Modality), three each:
    - Quantity: 1-many-all;
    - Quality: yes-no-limit;
    - Relation: substance-cause-community;
    - Modality: possible-actual-necessary.
- **Commonly confused pairs (categories)**

| Often confused | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Singular vs particular | The singular is one unique thing. The particular is the middle term, the general as embodied in a group or kind |
| Universal vs abstract idea | In diamat the universal is objective (common features of real things), discovered by generalisation |
| Essence vs substance | Substance is the ultimate bearer of all properties (ontology, §2). Essence is the inner, necessary side of a *given* thing, revealed through its phenomena |
| Phenomenon vs semblance | A phenomenon manifests essence. A semblance is a one-sided or distorted manifestation (essence in "one of its ... moments") |
| Kant's phenomenon/thing-in-itself vs diamat's phenomenon/essence | For Kant, things in themselves are unknowable (Engels counts him among the agnostics). In diamat, essence is knowable through phenomena and practice |
| Content–form vs essence–phenomenon | Content–form: composition vs organisation and boundary. Essence–phenomenon: inner basis vs outward manifestation |
| Cause vs condition vs occasion | The cause generates the effect. Conditions are necessary but cannot produce it. The occasion is only the external trigger |
| Chance = uncaused? | No. Chance events have causes. They are "external, contingent, inessential" for the given process |
| Necessity vs law | A law is a stable, repeated, essential, *necessary relation*. Necessity is the "impossibility of something being otherwise" |
| Formal vs real possibility | The first lacks sufficient conditions ("very remote"). The second is a law-governed tendency with conditions present |
| Possibility–reality vs potentiality–actuality | The diamat pair is close to Aristotle's, but Aristotle stresses the priority of actuality and his examples are teleological (acorn → oak) |
| Aristotle's vs Kant's categories | 10 kinds of *being* found by questions vs 12 a-priori concepts of the *understanding* derived from judgments |
| Kant's "quality" vs diamat's "quality" | Kant: reality, negation, limitation (forms of judgment). Diamat: the integral definiteness of a thing |
| Kant's "causality" heading | Causality belongs under **Relation**, not under Modality (Modality = possibility, existence, necessity) |

## 7. Methods: dialectics vs metaphysics; general scientific methods; empirical vs theoretical levels

### Takeaway
In Hegelian-Marxist usage, "dialectics vs metaphysics" contrasts two *methods* of thinking, not two disciplines:
- dialectics: connection, motion, development through leaps, internal contradiction;
- metaphysics: isolation, fixity, simple growth, "yea, yea; nay, nay".

Scientific cognition moves from the **empirical level** (observation, experiment, comparison, establishing and classifying facts) to the **theoretical level** (generalisation, hypothesis, theory, idealisation, ascent from the abstract to the concrete). General logical procedures are used at both levels: analysis and synthesis, abstraction and generalisation, induction and deduction, analogy, modelling. The historical and the logical methods are complementary: Engels calls the logical method "nothing but the historical method, only stripped of the historical form and diverting chance occurrences".

### Cited Findings

#### 7.1 Dialectics vs metaphysics (as methods)
- **Engels on the metaphysician:** "To the metaphysician, things and their mental reflexes, ideas, are isolated, are to be considered one after the other and apart from each other, are objects of investigation fixed, rigid, given once for all." The metaphysician thinks in "'yea, yea; nay, nay'; for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil" and "cannot see the woods for the trees". Engels traces this way of thinking to Bacon and Locke, who carried the isolating procedure of natural science into philosophy — [Engels, *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific*, ch. II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm)
- **Engels on dialectics:** it "comprehends things and their representations, ideas, in their essential connection, concatenation, motion, origin and ending". "Nature is the proof of dialectics" — [same](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm)
- **Stalin (1938) on the original sense of "dialectics":** "the art of arriving at the truth by disclosing the contradictions in the argument of an opponent and overcoming these contradictions" — [Stalin, "Dialectical and Historical Materialism"](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm)
- **Spirkin's principle of universal connection:** "Nothing in the world stands by itself. Every object is a link in an endless chain and is thus connected with all the other links" — [Spirkin 2.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s05.html)
- **Spirkin's principle of development.** Development is "a definitely oriented, irreversible change of the object, from the old to the new, from the simple to the complex, from a lower level to a higher one". Alongside ascending development there is regress: "degradation and decay of systems, descents from the higher to the lower" — [Spirkin 2.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s05.html)

| Feature | Dialectical method | Metaphysical method | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connection | "no phenomenon in nature can be understood if taken by itself, isolated from surrounding phenomena" | nature as "an accidental agglomeration of things ... unconnected with ... each other"; things "isolated ... apart from each other" | [Stalin 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm); [Engels SUS II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm) |
| Change | nature "in a state of continuous movement and change, of continuous renewal and development" | "a state of rest and immobility, stagnation and immutability"; things "fixed, rigid, given once for all" | [Stalin 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm); [Engels SUS II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm) |
| Development | from small quantitative changes to qualitative changes, coming "rapidly and abruptly, taking the form of a leap" | "simple growth, where quantitative changes do not lead to qualitative changes" | [Stalin 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm) |
| Source of development | internal contradictions: "the struggle between these opposites ... constitutes the internal content of the process of development" | (harmonious unfolding, external push) | [Stalin 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm) |
| Style of thought | things grasped "in their essential connection, concatenation, motion, origin and ending" | "yea, yea; nay, nay"; "cannot see the woods for the trees" | [Engels SUS II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm) |
| Historical carriers | Hegel (idealist dialectic); Marx and Engels (materialist dialectic); Heraclitus as the ancient forerunner | Bacon and Locke carried it into philosophy from natural science | [Engels SUS II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm); [uz.wikipedia](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialektika) |

#### 7.2 Knowledge and the forms of thought [SOV]
- **Knowledge** is "the result of the process of cognition of reality, tested by socio-historical practice and authenticated by logic, the true reflection of reality in human consciousness". Kinds of knowledge: pre-scientific (everyday), artistic, and scientific, which is itself empirical or theoretical — [Spirkin ch. 4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04.html)
- **Thought** is the "goal-oriented, mediated and generalised reflection of the significant properties and relations of things, the creative forming of new ideas, the posing and solving of problems" — [Spirkin 4.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html)
- **The three forms of thought:**
  - a *concept* is "a form of thought reflecting the essential properties, relations and connections of objects and phenomena in their contradictions and development";
  - a *judgment* is "a form of thought in which something is asserted or denied about something by linking up certain concepts";
  - an *inference* is "a process of reasoning in the course of which from one or several judgements, called premises, or assumptions, a new judgement (conclusion) is reached".
  
  — [Spirkin 4.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html)
- **Practice as the test.** Engels against agnosticism: "practice — namely, experiment and industry" — [Engels LF II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)

#### 7.3 General scientific methods: definitions table
(Definitions are quoted from [Spirkin 4.5 "Thought"](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html) unless another source is given. The "level" column and the examples are my classification, marked illustrative.)

| Method / operation | Definition | Level (my grouping) | Example (illustrative unless sourced) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | "an intentional, planned process of perception, carried out in order to identify the essential properties and relations" | empirical | logging Tashkent's daily air-quality readings |
| **Experiment** | "a method of research by which the object is artificially reproduced or placed in certain conditions that answer the needs of the researcher" | empirical | heating water at controlled pressures to find its boiling point |
| **Comparison** | "the mother of knowledge": establishing relationships between objects through resemblance and difference | empirical (and both) | comparing yields of two cotton varieties |
| **Analysis** | "the breaking-down of objects into their component parts or aspects, and this is done by both practical and theoretical work" | general logical (both levels) | taking an engine apart; splitting an argument into its premises |
| **Synthesis** | "the practical, mental integration of the elements that we broke it down into and examined" | general logical | reassembling the engine; writing the conclusion that unifies the findings |
| **Abstraction** | "the mental identification, singling out of some object from its connections with other objects, the separation of some attribute" | general logical, mostly theoretical | studying a body's mass while ignoring its colour |
| **Generalisation** | moving "from individual concepts to general concepts and from less general concepts to more general ones" | general logical | apple, pear → fruit → plant product |
| **Induction** | [IEP] an argument whose conclusion is "merely probable" given the premises; judged as strong or weak, cogent or not. [OpenStax] a strong induction is one where "if the evidence offered is true, then the conclusion is probably true" | from the empirical toward the theoretical | red-winged blackbirds return each March, so they generally return in March ([OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/5-4-types-of-inferences)) |
| **Deduction** | [IEP] an argument whose premises "logically entail" the conclusion; judged as valid or invalid, sound or unsound. [OpenStax] valid = "if the premises are true, then the conclusion must be true" | theoretical (derivation, prediction) | "All men are mortal; Socrates is a man; therefore Socrates is mortal" ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/deductive-inductive-arguments/)) |
| **Abduction** [WEST] | [OpenStax] "inference to the best explanation": from evidence to "the most likely explanation for that evidence" | hypothesis formation | the car won't start and the lights are off, so the battery is probably dead ([OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/5-4-types-of-inferences)) |
| **Analogy** | "an objective relationship between objects that makes it possible to apply the information gained through investigating one object to another object that is similar" | general logical | inferring a drug's effect in humans from its effect in similar mammals |
| **Idealisation** | forms concepts whose "real prototypes can be indicated only to a certain degree of approximation" | theoretical | "ideal gas", "material point" |
| **Modelling** | "the practical or theoretical replacement of the object of research by some natural or artificial analogue" | both | a wind-tunnel model of an aircraft; a computer climate model |
| **Hypothesis** | "a supposition based on facts, a starting-point for investigation of a part of reality that has not been sufficiently studied" | theoretical | Mendeleev's predicted "eka-aluminium" before gallium was found ([DoN II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch02.htm)) |
| **Theory** | "Unlike the hypothesis, the theory provides reliable knowledge (including reliable knowledge of the probability of certain events)" | theoretical | — |
| **Historical method** | describes development in its "concrete manifestation ... in all the infinite diversity of its specific and individual manifestations" | theoretical | a year-by-year history of a city |
| **Logical method** | expresses "the general line, the pattern of development"; "The logical is a generalised reflection of the historical" | theoretical | reconstructing the necessary stages of a process without its accidental details |
| **Ascent from the abstract to the concrete** | "the Marxist method of ascent from the abstract to the concrete": cognition "rolls forward from content to content", becoming "richer and more concentrated" | theoretical | — |

#### 7.4 Induction and deduction: precise view vs textbook slogan
- **IEP** (Timothy Shanahan). Deductive arguments are "those that make their premises logically entail its conclusion". Inductive arguments are those whose conclusions are "merely probable". The traditional idea that deduction goes "from general to particular" and induction "from particular to universal" fails on counterexamples: some arguments contain only universal statements, others only particular ones. Shanahan concludes that no categorical distinction is fully satisfactory — [IEP: Deductive and Inductive Arguments](https://iep.utm.edu/deductive-inductive-arguments/)
- **Evaluation.** Deductive arguments are valid or invalid, sound or unsound. Inductive arguments are strong or weak, cogent or not cogent — [IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/deductive-inductive-arguments/)
- **[SOV] Spirkin on two philosophical traditions.** "empiricism, which gave priority to experience and induction (Bacon and Locke)" vs "rationalism, a doctrine which regards abstract thought as the basis of human knowledge and gives priority to the deductive method" — [Spirkin 4.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html)

#### 7.5 The historical and the logical methods
- **Engels (1859).** "The logical method of approach was therefore the only suitable one. This, however, is indeed nothing but the historical method, only stripped of the historical form and diverting chance occurrences." And: "The point where this history begins must also be the starting point of the train of thought, and its further progress will be simply the reflection, in abstract and theoretically consistent form, of the historical course" — [Engels, review of Marx's *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy*, *Das Volk* No. 16, 20 August 1859](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/appx2.htm)
- **Engels on Hegel's merit here:** "the exceptional historical sense underlying Hegel's manner of reasoning" — [same](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/appx2.htm)
- **Spirkin.** "The logical method of research into the actual historical process is thus a matter of abstracting from the real historical process its intrinsic necessity" — [Spirkin 4.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html)

#### 7.6 Empirical vs theoretical levels
- **Spirkin.** "The motion of cognitive thought begins with the empirical, with the observation and establishing of facts, their analysis and classification, and goes on from there to their generalisation, the making of hypotheses ... and finally, the construction of theories" — [Spirkin 4.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html)
- **Spirkin on scientific knowledge:** it is "empirical and theoretical", as distinct from pre-scientific (everyday) and artistic knowledge — [Spirkin ch. 4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04.html)

### Inferences
- **A three-tier grouping that post-Soviet manuals typically use** (my reconstruction from the definitions above; the grouping itself is not quoted from a fetched source):

| Tier | Methods | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Empirical level | observation, comparison, (measurement), experiment, description | facts, data, empirical regularities |
| Theoretical level | idealisation, hypothesis, theory construction, (formalisation, axiomatic and hypothetico-deductive methods), historical and logical, ascent from the abstract to the concrete | laws, theories, explanations of *essence* |
| General logical (used at both levels) | analysis and synthesis, abstraction and generalisation, induction and deduction, analogy, modelling | the operations that move thought between the levels |

  Link to the categories: the empirical level stays mostly with *phenomena*, while the theoretical level reaches *essence* and *law*. This is the essence–phenomenon pair (§6) applied to knowledge.
- **Worked examples (illustrative).**
  - **Analysis → synthesis.** To understand Inha's timetable clash, break it into its parts (courses, rooms, time slots, instructors), then put them back together as a new timetable.
  - **Induction → hypothesis → deduction → experiment.**
    1. Observe that several students who sleep under 6 hours score lower (induction).
    2. Hypothesise that sleep deprivation lowers recall.
    3. Deduce a prediction: a well-rested group will out-score a sleep-deprived one on the same test.
    4. Test it by experiment.
    
    The loop runs empirical → theoretical → empirical.
  - **Historical vs logical.** A historical account of money lists every coin, note and reform in order. A logical account reconstructs only the necessary stages (barter → a general equivalent → money), dropping the "diverting chance occurrences" (Engels).
- **Commonly confused pairs (methods)**

| Often confused | Key difference |
|---|---|
| "Metaphysics" the discipline vs the method | The discipline: being qua being, ontology (§1). The method: an anti-dialectical way of thinking (Engels) |
| Ancient "dialectic" vs Hegelian-Marxist "dialectics" | The art of finding truth by exposing contradictions in an opponent's argument (the original sense, per Stalin) vs a theory and method of development |
| Analysis vs synthesis | Splitting into parts vs integrating the parts. They are complementary, not rivals |
| Induction vs deduction | Probable support vs logical entailment. "Particular → general / general → particular" is a classroom simplification that IEP rejects as inaccurate |
| Abstraction vs generalisation | Abstraction singles out one attribute or aspect. Generalisation moves to a broader concept or class |
| Abstraction vs idealisation | Idealisation builds limiting concepts with no exact real prototype (ideal gas) |
| Analogy vs modelling | Analogy is inference from similarity. Modelling replaces the object with an analogue that is then studied |
| Observation vs experiment | Planned perception without intervention vs artificially reproducing or controlling the conditions |
| Hypothesis vs theory | A supposition based on facts vs reliable, confirmed knowledge |
| Empirical level vs empiricism | A *level of knowledge* vs a *philosophical doctrine* that gives priority to experience and induction (Bacon, Locke) |
| Historical vs logical method | Not opposed: the logical is history "stripped of" its accidental form |
| Abduction | A standard Western category (inference to the best explanation) that is often absent from post-Soviet method lists |

#### 8.2 Twenty short-answer questions with model answers

**Theme 7: Ontology**

**SA1. What is ontology? Give its etymology, the history of the term, and the analytic reformulation.**
*Model answer:* Ontology is the philosophical study of being, "of what there is". The word comes from Greek *on/ontos* ("being") + *logos* ("discourse"). Its history:
- It is an early-modern coinage. Lorhard's *Ogdoas Scholastica* (1606) is the earliest printed use, and Goclenius' *Lexicon philosophicum* (1613) glosses it as "philosophia de ente".
- Clauberg (1647) spoke of "Ontosophia".
- Wolff's *Philosophia prima sive Ontologia* (1730) systematised it as general metaphysics, as opposed to cosmology, psychology and theology.
- Its ancestor is Aristotle's first philosophy, "the study of being qua being".

Analytic philosophy recasts ontology as a question of ontological commitment: Quine's "to be is to be the value of a variable". — [ontology.co](https://www.ontology.co/history.htm); [SEP: Ontological Commitment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ontological-commitment/); [SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)

**SA2. Name the four basic forms of being in post-Soviet textbooks, with their sub-forms.**
*Model answer:*
1. Being of things and processes (nature): first nature vs second, human-made nature.
2. Being of the human: in the world of things (as a body) and in society.
3. Being of the spiritual (ideal): individualised (personal consciousness) vs objectified (culture existing outside individuals).
4. Being of the social: individual being in society vs the being of society as a whole.

This follows the scheme in Frolov's *Introduction to Philosophy*. — [ru.wikipedia: Бытие](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5)

**SA3. State Engels' "basic question of philosophy" and its two sides. Define materialism, idealism and agnosticism.**
*Model answer:* The question is "the relation of thinking and being".
- *Side 1, what is primary?* Those who assert "the primacy of spirit to nature" form the camp of idealism; those who regard nature as primary are materialists.
- *Side 2, can our thinking know the world?* Agnostics (Hume, Kant) deny full knowability. Engels answers that "the most telling refutation ... is practice — namely, experiment and industry": chemists made alizarin dye from coal tar.

— [Engels, *Ludwig Feuerbach* II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)

**SA4. Distinguish subjective and objective idealism. Where does Kant fit?**
*Model answer:* Ontological idealism holds that "something mental ... is the ultimate foundation of all reality".
- *Subjective idealism* makes the individual perceiver's ideas primary: Berkeley's "esse is percipi".
- *Objective (absolute) idealism* grounds reality in a universal mind or absolute beyond individual consciousness (the later German idealists, Hegel).
- *Kant's transcendental idealism* makes space and time "features of our own minds" but keeps "empirical realism". Engels counts Kant among the agnostics.

— [SEP: Idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/); [Engels LF II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1886/ludwig-feuerbach/ch02.htm)

**SA5. Define substance and explain monism, dualism and pluralism, with one representative each.**
*Model answer:* Substance is "the vehicle, the bearer of all properties and relationships of everything that exists", persisting through change (Spirkin). For Aristotle, a substance is what is not predicated of anything else.
- *Monism* (one substance): Spinoza's God or Nature. Marxism is a materialist monism, with matter as substance.
- *Dualism* (two): Descartes' mind and body.
- *Pluralism* (many): Leibniz's monads.

— [Spirkin 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html); [SEP: Substance](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/substance/); [OpenStax 6.1](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/6-1-substance)

**SA6. Give Lenin's definition of matter and list the attributes of matter.**
*Model answer:* "Matter is a philosophical category denoting the objective reality which is given to man by his sensations, and which is copied, photographed and reflected by our sensations, while existing independently of them." The key attributes are "objective existence, structure, indestructibility, motion, space, time, reflection and information". The definition is epistemological: it defines matter through its independence from consciousness, not through any particular physical model. — [Spirkin 2.2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s02.html)

**SA7. Why is motion called "the mode of existence of matter", and why is rest relative?**
*Model answer:* Engels says motion, "the mode of existence, the inherent attribute of matter, comprehends all changes and processes occurring in the universe, from mere change of place right up to thinking", and is "as uncreatable as indestructible". Spirkin adds: "To be means to be in motion"; motion is *self*-motion. Rest is relative because "possessing absolute rest means ceasing to exist", and bodies rest "only in relation to a given system of reference". — [Engels DoN III](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch03.htm); [Spirkin 2.3](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s03.html)

**SA8. Compare the substantial and the relational conceptions of space and time. Which one does dialectical materialism adopt?**
*Model answer:*
- *Substantial (absolute)*: space and time are independent entities or containers. Newton defended absolute space in the *Principia* Scholium (bucket experiment, rotating globes).
- *Relational*: space is "an order of coexistence" and time "an order of succession" among things (Leibniz). Mach later attacked absolute space.

Diamat takes the relational side, with objectivity: space is "a form of coordination of coexisting objects", time a form of coordination "in their succession", and both are "universal forms of the existence of matter". After relativity, "with the disappearance of matter space and time would also disappear". Space is three-dimensional; time is one-dimensional and irreversible. — [SEP: Absolute and Relational Space and Motion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories/); [Spirkin 2.4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s04.html)

**SA9. Contrast Parmenides and Heraclitus on being and change.**
*Model answer:*
- *Parmenides* (Elea): there are two ways, "it is" and "it is not", and only the first is passable. What is is "ungenerated and deathless, whole and uniform, and still and perfect". Change and plurality are not genuinely real, and the senses deceive. The Russian textbook slogan: "Being is; non-being is not at all".
- *Heraclitus* (Ephesus): "On those stepping into rivers staying the same other and other waters flow" (B12), i.e., identity through constant change. Opposites form a unity ("living and dead, waking and sleeping, young and old"), and the world is an "everliving fire, kindling in measures and being quenched in measures".

Parmenides founds ontology and the static view of being; Heraclitus is the forerunner of dialectics. — [SEP: Parmenides](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parmenides/); [SEP: Heraclitus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/); [ru.wikipedia](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5); [uz.wikipedia](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialektika)

**Theme 8: Laws, categories, methods**

**SA10. State Engels' three laws of dialectics and where each appears in Hegel's *Logic*.**
*Model answer:*
1. The transformation of quantity into quality and vice versa: in the Doctrine of Being.
2. The interpenetration of opposites: it fills the Doctrine of Essence.
3. The negation of the negation: "the fundamental law for the construction of the whole system".

Hegel had them only "as mere laws of *thought*". Engels "turned them round" into laws of nature, history and thought, and defined dialectics as "the science of the general laws of motion and development of nature, human society and thought". — [DoN II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1883/don/ch02.htm); [Anti-Dühring XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm)

**SA11. Define quality, quantity, measure and leap, and explain the law of transition of quantity into quality with the water example.**
*Model answer:*
- *Quality* is the integral definiteness of an object: "the functional unity of an object's essential properties".
- *Quantity* is its "external, formal" side: number, size, degree.
- *Measure* is "the quantitative limit of a given quality".
- A *leap* is "a resolving of contradictions", and it can be momentary or gradual.

The law: accumulated quantitative changes exceed the measure, which produces a leap to a new quality. Engels' example: water at normal pressure turns solid at 0 °C and gaseous at 100 °C, so "at both these turning-points the merely quantitative change of temperature brings about a qualitative change". — [Spirkin 2.9](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s09.html); [Anti-Dühring XII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch10.htm)

**SA12. What is dialectical negation? Explain the negation of the negation with Engels' barley example.**
*Model answer:* Dialectical negation "does not mean simply saying no ... or destroying it in any way one likes". One must "not only negate, but also sublate the negation", i.e., cancel and preserve at once (Hegel's *aufheben*).
- *Barley:* the grain "ceases to exist" and the plant appears (first negation). The plant produces grain again, "not as a single unit, but ten-, twenty- or thirtyfold" (the negation of the negation).
- *Not dialectical:* grinding the grain, because it makes the second negation impossible.

Development is therefore "not a straight line and not motion in a circle, but a spiral". — [Anti-Dühring XIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch11.htm); [Spirkin 2.10](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s10.html); [SEP Hegel](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)

**SA13. Explain the law of unity and struggle of opposites, including Lenin's thesis on unity and struggle.**
*Model answer:* Every whole contains opposite sides that presuppose and exclude each other: "the extreme case of difference is an opposite". Their unity-and-struggle is a dialectical contradiction, which "awakens every seed to growth".
- Lenin: "The splitting of a single whole and the cognition of its contradictory parts ... is the essence of dialectics".
- His examples: + and −; action and reaction; positive and negative electricity; combination and dissociation of atoms; class struggle.
- "The unity ... of opposites is conditional, temporary, transitory, relative. The struggle of mutually exclusive opposites is absolute."

— [Spirkin 2.11](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s11.html); [Lenin 1915](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm)

**SA14. Which question about development does each law answer?**
*Model answer:*
- *Unity and struggle of opposites → SOURCE (why).* Contradictions are "internal sources of development", the key to "self-movement".
- *Quantity into quality → MECHANISM (how).* Development "takes place through the accumulation of quantitative changes ... going beyond the measure and a leap-like transition to a new quality".
- *Negation of the negation → DIRECTION/FORM (where to).* It "reflects ... directionality" and a spiral form.

— [ru.wikipedia: unity law](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B0_%D0%B8_%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%8C%D0%B1%D1%8B_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%B9); [ru.wikipedia: quantity law](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B0_%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D1%8B%D1%85_%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%B2_%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D1%8B%D0%B5); [ru.wikipedia: negation law](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F_%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F); [Lenin 1915](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm)

**SA15. Define the singular, the particular and the universal, and give an example.**
*Model answer:*
- The *singular* is "an object taken in its distinctness from everything else and in its unique specific" character (no two maple leaves are identical).
- The *particular* is "the realised general", the general embodied in a group.
- The *universal* is "the singular in the many", found by generalisation and named by common nouns.

Lenin: in "Ivan is a man", "the individual is the universal". Example (illustrative): Tashkent (singular) → the cities of Central Asia (particular) → city (universal). — [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html); [Lenin 1915](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1915/misc/x02.htm)

**SA16. Distinguish cause, condition and occasion, with an example.**
*Model answer:*
- *Causality* is "a genetic connection of phenomena through which one thing (the cause) under certain conditions gives rise to ... something else (the effect)". The cause is "active and primary".
- *Conditions* "cannot in themselves give rise to the effect, but the cause is also powerless without them".
- An *occasion* is "the external push or circumstance that sets in motion a train of underlying interconnections" (Spirkin: a head cold that triggers an underlying illness).

Example (illustrative): a short circuit (cause) ignites a fire given oxygen and flammable materials (conditions) when someone switches on an overloaded heater (occasion). For contrast, Aristotle's four causes are material, formal, efficient and final. — [Spirkin 2.6](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s06.html); [SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)

**SA17. Explain the dialectic of necessity and chance, using Engels' Napoleon example.**
*Model answer:*
- *Necessity* is "the impossibility of something being otherwise".
- *Chance* covers "external, contingent, inessential events", yet it is "a form of manifestation of necessity itself": "the necessary carves a road for itself through an infinite number of accidents".
- Engels: that Napoleon, "just that particular Corsican", became dictator was an accident, but "if a Napoleon had been lacking, another would have filled the place" (Caesar, Augustus, Cromwell). Society is governed by necessity "which is supplemented by and appears under the forms of accident".

— [Spirkin 2.8](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch02-s08.html); [Engels to Borgius, 1894](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894/letters/94_01_25.htm)

**SA18. Compare Aristotle's 10 categories with Kant's 12.**
*Model answer:*
- *Aristotle* listed the highest kinds of being: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, date, posture, state, action, passion. He found them by asking which questions can be put to a thing; primary substances (individuals) are fundamental.
- *Kant* derived 12 a-priori concepts of the understanding from the forms of judgment, under four headings:
  - Quantity: unity, plurality, totality;
  - Quality: reality, negation, limitation;
  - Relation: substance/accident, cause/effect, community;
  - Modality: possibility, existence, necessity.
  
  They apply to "all objects of possible cognition".
- Analytic philosophy asks whether categories carve reality (realism) or only our conceptual scheme (descriptivism).

— [SEP: Categories](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/categories/)

**SA19. Contrast the dialectical and the metaphysical methods.**
*Model answer:* For the metaphysician (Engels), things are "isolated ... fixed, rigid, given once for all", and thinking runs in "yea, yea; nay, nay"; Bacon and Locke carried this into philosophy. Dialectics grasps things "in their essential connection, concatenation, motion, origin and ending". Stalin's four contrasts (1938):
1. connection vs isolation;
2. motion and change vs rest;
3. qualitative leaps vs simple growth;
4. internal contradictions vs harmony.

Note that "metaphysics" here means a method, not the discipline of ontology. — [Engels SUS II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1880/soc-utop/ch02.htm); [Stalin 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1938/09.htm)

**SA20. Explain the empirical and theoretical levels of knowledge, and how induction and deduction differ.**
*Model answer:* Cognition "begins with the empirical, with the observation and establishing of facts, their analysis and classification, and goes on from there to their generalisation, the making of hypotheses ... and finally, the construction of theories". A theory, unlike a hypothesis, "provides reliable knowledge".
- *Deduction*: the premises "logically entail" the conclusion (valid or invalid; sound or unsound).
- *Induction*: the premises make the conclusion "merely probable" (strong or weak; cogent or not).
- IEP warns that "general → particular / particular → general" is an inaccurate slogan.
- Historically, empiricism (Bacon, Locke) favoured induction and rationalism favoured deduction.

— [Spirkin 4.5](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/spirkin/works/dialectical-materialism/ch04-s05.html); [IEP: Deductive and Inductive Arguments](https://iep.utm.edu/deductive-inductive-arguments/)

    - each category pair's definition plus one example;
    - dialectics vs metaphysics;
    - method definitions;
    - the empirical vs theoretical levels.
- **Likely MCQ traps.**
  - "Metaphysics" as a method vs as a discipline.
  - Thesis–antithesis–synthesis attributed to Hegel.
  - Leaps assumed always sudden.
  - "Chance = no cause".
  - Kant's causality placed under modality.
  - Measure confused with quantity.
  - Agnosticism confused with idealism.
  - Monism confused with materialism.
  - Relational space read as "subjective".
  - Lorhard vs Goclenius vs Wolff.
  - Unity and struggle mixed up (which is absolute? struggle).
- **Answer template for "explain law X" questions** (it scores well because it covers every part):
  1. state the law;
  2. define its key concepts;
  3. give a classic example (Engels);
  4. give your own example;
  5. say which question about development it answers (source / mechanism / direction).
- **Answer template for "explain category pair X/Y" questions:**
  1. define both sides;
  2. state their relation (which side determines, and how they pass into each other);
  3. give an example;
  4. name one confusion to avoid.
