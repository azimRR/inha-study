                                                SUMMARY LEAD
• The summary lead tells the most important informa on
  about a situa on or event.
• It tells the results, or the climax, of the story.
• It answers the ques ons: Who? What? Where? When
• Usually it tries to answer: How? Why?
• The summary lead is the equivalent of a thesis in a
  scholarly paper, except that in news, it appears as the rst
  sentence of the story.
• The summary lead does not include every minute detail.
• The details are saved for the body of the story.

                   PARTIAL SUMMARY LEAD
• The par al summary lead, o en used in feature stories
  and news stories emphasizing a par cular angle, omits a
  few details.
• In some cases, inclusion of all the major points would
  create a crowded and/or confusing lead.
• In other cases, the writer wants to create suspense.
• The writer sets a tone by the language she chooses that
  gives readers another clue as to what the story is about. Is
  it about something serious? A murder? Is it lighthearted?
  Another stupid criminal does something stupid?

                      MULTIPLE ELEMENT & ROUNDUP LEADS
• The best approach is to iden fy what the elements have
  in common and create a main clause re ec ng that point.
• Then group the other aspects, iden fy them by key words
  or phrases, priori ze the more important aspects and
  create subordinate phrases.
• Think of the lead as the table of contents for the rest of
  the story.
• Another approach is to priori ze the elements, deal with
  the most important in a summary lead, and then write a
  second paragraph that summarizes the other points.

  many ways to approach any event or situa on.

                                          FOLLOW UP LEAD
• The follow up lead, like that of any news story,
