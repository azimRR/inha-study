Is the first reader breaking the rule?
Never sleep holding the key — yet he can sleep on board
while holding mutex. Allowed here: whoever holds the
board (the cook) never asks for mutex, so no circle of
waiting can form. The rule is a safety habit; the real test is
always the circle: who waits for whom.

## Page 7

Starvation, Measured
10/18/26
SOC 3010              j.yusupov@inha.uz
Synchronisation II: Readers and Writers
Correct, and still unfair
The lightswitch never lets the cook and a reader share the
board. In 8 of 8 runs (3 shown) the cook never rewrote the
menu (writes 0). He came out at 1 s and waited up to 10.0 s
— he got the board only after closing time (10 s).
Starvation
A process that is never chosen although the program is
correct and everybody else keeps moving. Not a deadlock:
the café is busy — only the cook never gets the chalk.
The arithmetic
The board is unlocked only when readers returns to
0. If a new reader comes in before the last one
leaves, it never does: the readers keep the board for
ever, and W stays asleep on board.

## Page 8

Why it works
1. A waiting writer takes the
turnstile and keeps it while it sleeps
on board: the gate is closed.
2. Readers already inside drain out.
3. New readers queue at the gate.
4. The last one out posts board:
the writer writes, then opens the
gate.
The Turnstile Fix
10/18/26
SOC 3010              j.yusupov@inha.uz
Synchronisation II: Readers and Writers
What the gate costs
Without the gate (8 runs): 24
reads, 0 writes, the cook waits up
to 10.0 s. With it (8 runs): 14–18
reads, 2–3 writes, at most 2.0 s —
about one reading time (2 s). The
price: a little less reading.
